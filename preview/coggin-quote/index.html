1.The short version

The Coggin Firm has fifty years of experience, two offices, a former District Court Judge, and a managing partner who chairs the state association's workers' compensation section. Almost none of that is visible in the first ten seconds of the current website.

We have already built the redesign. Rather than send a proposal describing what we might do, the homepage, the practice-area template, both attorney profiles and the contact experience are live and clickable at williamsdigital.io/preview/coggin-firm. Open it on your phone — that is where most people will find you.

This quote covers finishing that work into a complete, launched website: $1,500 one time, and $50 per month for hosting, the consultation-form automation, and ongoing upkeep.

2.What we found on the current site

These are observations from the live site, not criticism of the firm. Every one of them is fixable, and most are invisible to you but very visible to Google.

01The homepage does not say what you doA visitor has to scroll past a slider before learning that you handle injuries, workplace claims, disability, family matters, criminal defense and estates — or that there are two offices.
02Twelve practice areas presented as a flat listNo grouping, no one-line descriptions. Someone who was hurt at work has to already know the phrase "workers' compensation" to find the right page.
03Legacy URLs that mean nothingWorkers' compensation currently lives at /practice-areas/241-2/ and Social Security at /?p=273. Search engines and people both read those as noise.
04Internal links still use http://Several practice-area links point at insecure http:// addresses rather than https://.
05Attorney stories buried in long text blocksThe material is genuinely good — fifteen years on the district court bench, a pilot program prosecuting child sex crimes, the ALAJ Al Sansone Award. It reads as one dense paragraph.
06The two-office structure is not obviousGadsden and Centre serve two different counties. Nothing on the site helps someone in Cherokee County understand you are local to them.
07A conflict in the judicial titleOne section describes John Coggin as a District Court Judge and another as a Circuit Court Judge. His own biography says District Court, appointed December 1982, served until 1997 — that is what our rebuild uses throughout. Worth one confirmation from you before launch.
How we handled the facts Every address, phone number, fax, credential and quotation in the preview was taken from your own website, not invented. Where something could not be confirmed — office hours, whether awards may be used in advertising, whether estate work includes conservatorships — we left it out and listed it for you to confirm rather than guessing. You will not find a single fabricated statistic, testimonial or case result anywhere in the preview.

3.What gets built

Homepage, desktop and mobile
Two deliberately different layouts, not one design squeezed down. Mobile leads with the phone number and reorders the page for someone deciding whether to call.
Twelve practice-area pages
One consistent template: what you handle, common situations, what to do next, FAQs, the attorney responsible, and a consultation prompt that follows down the page.
Two attorney profiles
Your existing biographies rewritten into scannable sections — experience timeline, education, admissions, recognition — without losing the personal tone.
Two location pages
Gadsden and Centre each get their own page with map, directions, parking, hours, and the local search relevance that comes with it.
Consultation flow
A short, plain-language form with an explicit instruction not to send confidential details, correct mobile keyboards, real error messages, and a confirmation the sender can trust.
Logo cleanup
Your "C" mark redrawn as scalable vector artwork in horizontal, stacked, reversed and one-colour versions. The current site uses a small raster file.
Content rewrite and migration
All existing material carried across and rewritten for hierarchy and legibility. Nothing of substance is lost; nothing new is claimed without your approval.
Redirect map
A permanent 301 redirect from every existing URL to its new home, plus http to https. Nobody lands on a dead page and no search ranking is thrown away.
Local search foundations
Unique titles and descriptions per page, clean URLs, structured data for a legal practice, both offices and both attorneys, sitemap, and Search Console setup.
Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA
Keyboard operation, visible focus, correct headings, labelled forms, verified colour contrast, and respect for reduced-motion settings. Built in from the start, not bolted on.
Speed and Core Web Vitals
Modern image formats, minimal scripts, no sliders or auto-playing media, reserved image dimensions so nothing jumps while loading.
Editing access for your staff
Approved staff can update attorney details, office information, hours and FAQs without calling us.

4.Not included

  • Photography. Attorney portraits and office exteriors are the single biggest visual upgrade available to you, and the preview leaves labelled placeholders for them. A local half-day shoot is quoted separately, or you supply your own images.
  • Legal review of the policy pages. We draft the privacy, disclaimer and accessibility language; you or your counsel approve it, including any attorney-advertising wording the Alabama State Bar requires.
  • A secure client portal or document intake. Deliberately out of scope. The consultation form is designed to carry a general description only, never case documents or medical records.
  • Ongoing article writing. The launch content above is included. A regular articles programme is a separate arrangement if you want one later.
  • Paid advertising, directory management and review generation. Not part of this quote.
  • Domain registration. You already own thecogginfirm.com and it stays in your name. We point it at the new site at launch; renewal stays with you.
  • Third-party costs — premium fonts, licensed stock imagery, paid plugins — billed at cost only if you approve them first.

5.How it runs

Phase 1 — about one week
Discovery and content verification
We sit down with the confirmation list: credentials, admissions, awards and whether they can be advertised, office hours, fee language, and which practice areas matter most to the firm. You approve the sitemap.
Phase 2 — largely complete
Design system and key pages
Palette, typography and components are already built and visible in the preview. This phase is your feedback on them, not a blank page.
Phase 3 — two to three weeks
Build and content migration
The remaining eleven practice areas, both location pages, the resources section and the policy pages. Forms, analytics, structured data and the redirect map are configured here.
Phase 4 — about one week
Quality assurance
Phones, tablets and desktop browsers. Accessibility, performance, form delivery, link and SEO checks. You proofread every legal and biographical statement before anything goes live.
Phase 5 — launch, then 30–60 days
Launch and follow-up
DNS cutover, sitemap submitted, redirects and form delivery monitored. We review real visitor behaviour after a month or two and adjust the calls to action and page order accordingly.

Roughly four to six weeks from the deposit and materials being received. The largest variable is how quickly the confirmation list in Phase 1 comes back, since we will not publish an unverified credential.
