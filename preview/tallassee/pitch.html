The Opportunity

Four things holding the current site back

01

The homepage is cluttered

Links, notices, and menus compete with no hierarchy. Residents come to do one thing — pay a utility bill, find an agenda — and have to hunt for it.

02

The story is invisible

One of Alabama's great mill towns, the Tallapoosa, Thurlow Dam, the Lake Martin gateway — genuinely marketable identity that never appears in a compelling way online.

03

Relocation info doesn't exist

Tallassee's best growth argument — corridor location, housing value, city schools — isn't presented anywhere. Families comparing towns never see the case.

04

The visuals feel dated

Small images, inconsistent spacing, old-style layouts. Perception matters — to residents, to visitors headed to the lake, and to businesses comparing Tallassee against Wetumpka or Millbrook.

More Than a Facelift

What a modern municipal site actually delivers

The visible redesign is the easy part. These are the points that matter to stakeholders, auditors, and grant applications:

ADA Accessible

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA — screen-reader friendly, keyboard navigable, proper contrast. Government sites are held to this standard, and non-compliance is a legal risk.

Mobile First

Most residents reach city services from a phone. Every page is designed for a phone screen first, desktop second.

Fast Loading

Lightweight pages that load in under two seconds — including on rural Elmore and Tallapoosa County connections.

Staff-Editable

City staff post news, alerts, and events themselves — no developer, no waiting. Dam release notices go up in minutes.

The Concept

What we'd build — see it live

We've already designed a working homepage concept for Tallassee, built around the growth-story model:

  • Hero: the mill on the river — "Built By The River. Growing Toward The Future."
  • "Tallassee Then & Now" — a drag-to-compare slider between the mill era and today's downtown
  • "Tallassee Through Time" — five-chapter storytelling: River Origins, Mill Era, Downtown Growth, Modern Tallassee, Future Vision
  • Downtown Spotlight — featured restaurants and shops, plus a rotating Business of the Month
  • Community Pulse — council, events, school activities, road projects, new businesses in simple cards
  • Live Growth Tracker — projects and infrastructure with real progress
  • "Why Tallassee?" — the growth-corridor case plus an interactive relocation comparison
  • A prominent "Moving to Tallassee" section — schools, utilities, neighborhoods, Auburn proximity, new resident guide

The Payoff

Benefits for the City

Beyond the new look, the redesign changes how the city operates day to day:

  • Mobile-friendly for residents — services work from any phone
  • Faster access to city services — pay, report, and apply in fewer clicks
  • Easier navigation for permits and forms — less counter traffic, fewer calls to City Hall
  • ADA-conscious design — accessible to every resident, and defensible
  • A relocation pitch working 24/7 — the corridor-family case made for you
  • Growth made visible — projects and progress residents can point to
  • Improved community engagement — events, alerts, and council business residents actually see
Helping Tallassee Grow

An investment that works for the city

A municipal website isn't an expense line — it's infrastructure for the four things every corridor town is trying to do:

Family Relocation

The Auburn–Montgomery corridor is growing. A city that makes its case — housing, schools, commute — captures families the metros price out.

Business Attraction

A credible downtown and business hub with available properties and a named contact — the first impression every relocating business checks.

Community Engagement

Events, meetings, and city news residents actually find — participation goes up when information is easy.

Resident Services

Bills paid, issues reported, permits started online — around the clock, without a trip to City Hall.
