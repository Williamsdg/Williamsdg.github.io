PREPARED BY WILLIAMS DIGITAL — seal and logo options for the Town of Decatur, Mississippi. Concepts for discussion, not adopted marks.

Town of Decatur · Newton County, Mississippi

Built from the flag the Town already has.

Decatur has a flag carrying its motto, a legal duty to hold a seal it does not appear to own, and a blank circle on its police cars where that seal belongs. Every option here is drawn in the flag’s own gold and black, and every one carries Faith · Family · Education.

The case

Three facts, and one of them is the law

The flag exists

A gold, white and gold triband reading DECATUR over “Committed to Faith · Family · Education.” Recorded by Flags of the World and photographed at a Mississippi Municipal League conference. It is not on the Town’s website, and its motto line matches the Town’s former site exactly.

There is no seal

Neither the Town nor Newton County has one. The centre of the Town’s police vehicle badge is a blank gold disc on a stock catalogue decal — a ready-made slot.

Mississippi requires one

§ 21-15-17: “each municipality shall adopt and provide a seal,” with the clerk as its custodian. Courts have held an ordinance inadmissible without it. No design is prescribed.

Why that matters to the Board: this is a compliance item with a brand benefit, not a refresh for its own sake. The seal is also legally operative on cheques and warrants, bonds, tax-redemption releases, and anything the Mayor executes.
And one open slot nobody is using. Decatur has two water towers. Both are plain white and neither carries a single letter.

The options

Ten directions, all from the flag

Each is shown large, then at 48 px and 26 px — because a seal that only works big is not a seal. All are vector, all carry the motto, and each ships in a one-colour version for embossing and embroidery.

01

The Great Seal

Recommended for the seal

Town name in the upper ring, motto in the lower, the arch and 1836 at the centre.

Why: This is the one that behaves like a legal seal. It is the right shape for an embosser, it reads at three-quarters of an inch, and it says everything a Mississippi seal has to say.

02

The Flag Seal

Most distinctly Decatur

The Town flag itself, set inside a ring carrying the name and the motto.

Why: Nobody else can use this. It takes the flag the Town already has and makes it work as a seal, which also means the flag stops being forgotten.

03

Triband Shield

Strong

The flag’s gold-white-gold bands, shield-shaped, with the motto across the base.

Why: Reads instantly at any size and sits naturally on a vehicle door or a uniform.

04

Arch & Banner

Strong

The arch under the town name, with the motto on a ribbon.

Why: The arch recurs on the welcome monument, the 1912 courthouse, the 1913 jail parapet and the 1930 gymnasium. It is the one form Decatur keeps building.

05

The Oak Roundel

Warm

The oak from the mural and the welcome sign, ringed by the name and the motto.

Why: The friendliest of the set. Drawn as an inland oak — a live oak would be wrong this far from the coast.

06

Civic Badge

Built for the door

A shield with the courthouse and the motto on a ribbon.

Why: Shaped for exactly the gap that exists today: the blank medallion on the Town’s police decal.

07

The Commodore

Distinctive

A gold star for Stephen Decatur, the naval officer the town is named for.

Why: Scales smaller than anything else here and is unmistakably about this name. Nobody local uses it.

08

Brick D

Modern

A monogram built from brick courses, motto beneath.

Why: Every courthouse Decatur lost was rebuilt in brick. “NC” belongs to the high school; “D” is free.

09

The Flag, Straight

Simplest

The flag drawn as the identity, motto and all.

Why: If the Town wants the least possible change, this is it — the flag it already has, drawn properly and made usable.

10

Three Virtues

Works big

The motto as three images and three words — the mural, condensed.

Why: Honest caveat: this one is handsome on a sign or a wall and turns to mud at small sizes. Good as a secondary mark, not as the seal.

A note on the gold. The flag as vectorised uses a brighter lemon gold. These marks use a slightly deeper gold so that black type on it stays readable and it survives printing and embroidery. If the Town wants the flag’s exact shade, that is an easy change — it simply needs to be a decision rather than a drift.
Worth saying out loud: gold and black is also East Central Community College’s palette, and ECCC is headquartered in Decatur. Building on the flag is defensible precisely because the flag is the Town’s own and came first — but the Board should make that choice knowingly rather than be surprised by the comparison later.

Guardrails

What must not go in

  • No Choctaw-derived motifs without the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians’ written consent. Conehatta, in Newton County, is one of the Tribe’s reservation communities.
  • No likeness of Medgar or Charles Evers. Both were born in Decatur and the marker stands on the courthouse lawn. Honour it in narrative and signage — not in a mark, and not without the family’s blessing.
  • Nothing from East Central Community College — the Warrior head, the “EC” monogram, the arch logo or the wordmark.
  • No school marks — the Cougar paw and “NC” monogram, or the Academy’s star-through-N.
  • No state flag or Great Seal device, and no federal insignia.
  • No live oak, no Spanish moss. That species grows only in coastal Mississippi — Newton County is around 150 miles inland.
  • No cotton. Newton County ranked 36th of 60 in 1860 and moved to corn. Crops are three per cent of farm sales today; poultry is ninety-three.
  • Nothing Confederate. Decatur’s courthouse lawn carries a Medgar Evers marker and an all-veterans memorial, and no Confederate monument.

Process

How a Mississippi town adopts one

1. Ask the clerk first

Whether a seal was ever adopted by ordinance, and when the flag was adopted. The Town’s ordinances are not published anywhere online and the clerk is the statutory custodian.

2. Show the Board the blank badge

Pair the photograph with § 21-15-17. One image makes the case.

3. Take a little public input

Even a short comment period. “We were never asked” is what sinks these elsewhere.

4. Adopt by ordinance, on the minutes

Artwork as an exhibit, the clerk named custodian, a short use policy, and a clause protecting documents already sealed.

5. Two marks, not one

A legally correct seal the clerk uses on cheques and certified copies, and a logo for everything the public sees.

6. Phase the rollout

Signs and vehicles as they come up for replacement. Nothing budgeted for merchandise in year one.

Two things to verify before anything is adopted. That the clerk confirms no seal already exists — nothing here should be designed over the top of something the Town already owns. And a clearance search, so the chosen mark is not close to something already registered.
