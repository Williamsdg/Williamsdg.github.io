Pre-Launch Checklist

Everything that has to happen before the new site goes live on townofdecaturms.gov — who does each piece, and which items can hold up a launch if they are left too late.

Nothing here is difficult, and most of it is answering a question rather than doing a task. It is written out so that nothing gets discovered late. The items marked Town are the only ones that need someone at Town Hall; everything else is Williams Digital's job.

Two items are worth starting now because they depend on other people: domain access and the billing software question. Both are explained below.

1. Before the build starts Week 1

These five things let the work begin. None of them take long.

2. Content the Town supplies Town

The bones of the site are already built. What is left is making sure every word on it is true, and putting the Town's own pictures on it instead of placeholder ones.

3. The domain Start this now

This is the one item that can genuinely delay a launch, and it is worth settling regardless of whether the Town goes ahead with this project at all.

Why this one is first on the list

Without the ability to change a DNS setting, a finished website cannot be pointed at townofdecaturms.gov — it simply sits on a preview link. Everything else can be fixed in an afternoon. This one depends on another company responding, so it should be started before it is needed.

4. The resident portal Option B only

Skip this section entirely if the Town is starting with the public website alone.

If the billing software cannot export

Four features — balance, due date, usage history and the high-usage leak warning — depend on that export. If the answer is no, those are not built, and the price drops by $400 one-time and $10 per month. Everything else in the portal works either way. This is why the question is worth asking in week one.

5. Williams Digital's side No action needed from the Town

Listed so the Town can see what is being done on its behalf, not because anyone at Town Hall needs to do it.

6. Training, before anything goes live Week 2

Whoever will actually use the editor should be on this, not just the Mayor.

Go-live day

The switch itself takes minutes. It is scheduled for a weekday morning, never a Friday afternoon, so that if anything needs attention there is a whole working day to handle it.

  1. Balance paid — the remaining half of the one-time fee.
  2. Final approval — the Town's point of contact confirms the site is right.
  3. DNS changed — the domain is pointed at the new site. Visitors may see the old site for a few hours while this spreads; both work during that window, so nobody hits a dead page.
  4. Checked live — every page, the documents, the forms, and the padlock, on both a computer and a phone.
  5. The Town announces it — a note on the Town's Facebook page and a line in the next utility bill reaches most residents.
  6. Monthly service begins 30 days later — not on launch day. The first month is on the house.
