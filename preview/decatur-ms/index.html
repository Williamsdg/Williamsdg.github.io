Born here

Medgar and Charles Evers were born in Decatur.

Charles in 1922, Medgar in 1925. In 1946, home from the war, the brothers walked to the courthouse in this town to register to vote and were turned away. Medgar went on to become Mississippi’s first NAACP field secretary. Charles became the first African-American mayor elected in Mississippi since Reconstruction.

A Mississippi Department of Archives and History marker stands on West Broad Street.

This is national history, and it belongs to Decatur. The current website does not mention it anywhere.

Since 1836

Seven courthouses. Five fires. One town that never let go of the county seat.

Decatur’s history is the single best piece of writing on the current website — and it sits three clicks deep on a page nobody finds. It belongs out front.

1836
Newton County is formed — and Decatur wins the courthouse

Carved from Neshoba County in February. Decatur is chosen as the county seat, and lots are auctioned for homes and businesses as soon as the courthouse and jail sites are settled. Three separate attempts were later made to move the courthouse elsewhere in the county. Decatur won the vote every time.

1864
Sherman comes through

In February, on the march to Meridian, Sherman’s army stopped at Decatur and burned the courthouse along with homes, churches, and businesses. The people of Decatur rebuilt.

1876
Forty years of records, gone in a night

The courthouse burned again in September, in the middle of a term of circuit court, taking nearly every deed and land record the county had. Court finished that term in the Baptist church.

1912
The Village becomes the Town

On October 10, the Newton County Times published Acting Governor Theo. G. Bilbo’s proclamation: the Village of Decatur, population 324, was now the Town of Decatur.

1928
The town buys itself a college

To win the county’s agricultural high school, Decatur put up land, buildings, and cash worth about $12,000. The first freshman class arrived in September 1928. That school is now East Central Community College — and the town’s population doubled in the decade it opened.

1934
The Legion Hut is raised

American Legion Post No. 89, founded by World War I veterans in 1920, rebuilt its hall after a fire — horizontal log walls, exposed trusses, and two ironstone fireplaces. It is the only building in Decatur on the National Register of Historic Places, and it is still in use.

Drawn from the Town of Decatur’s own published history and the 1894 History of Newton County, Mississippi.
