Town of Decatur, Mississippi

Town website and resident portal

A brief for the Mayor and Board of Aldermen · prepared by Williams Digital
See the proposed site ↗ See the resident portal ↗ Six pages. Written to be read aloud or handed round.

In one paragraph

The Town would replace its public website and add something it does not have at all today — a resident portal, where people can pay a bill, report a problem and follow what happens next. Nothing about how the Town runs its billing or its records would change. The Town keeps the systems it already uses. The cost is $2,500 once to build it, and $100 a month thereafter, against the $720 a year the Town pays now for the website alone.

What residents would get that they cannot get today

The Town currently has a website. It does not have a portal.

One place to pay

Water bill and court fine reached from the same page, instead of a resident needing to know which system handles which.

Report a problem, and see it move

A water leak, a pothole, a street light. The resident gets a number and a text as it progresses — rather than a phone call that leaves no record, or a comment on Facebook.

Cut-off dates published ahead

Twelve months of due dates and disconnection dates, posted in advance, so fewer residents are caught out and fewer of those calls reach Town Hall.

Emergency notices that reach people

A boil water notice appears at the top of every page, and can go out by text and email at the same time — not only to the people who happen to check Facebook.

Ordinances and forms that open properly

All 54 ordinances as direct links to the document, and forms residents can find without calling to ask.

Answers at 7 p.m.

Town Hall closes at 5:00 and for an hour at lunch. Most of what residents call about could be answered without a call at all.

What would not change

This is the important part, and it is deliberate.

  • The Town keeps BBI. Billing, administration and the Town’s records stay exactly where they are. Nothing is migrated.
  • Payments keep running through the Town’s existing processors. No card details would ever pass through the new system.
  • Municipal court stays as it is. Residents would pay by citation number only. There would be no public court docket and no name search.
  • The minute book remains the official record. Nothing online replaces it.
  • Newspaper publication is unaffected. Ordinances, the budget and the audit synopsis continue to take legal effect through the newspaper, as state law requires. The website supplements that; it never substitutes for it.
  • No staff retraining on a new administrative system. Staff would learn one thing: how to edit the website.
Put plainly: this sits on top of how the Town already works. It is a better front door, not a different building.

What it would save the Town

No dollar figure is claimed. These are the places the time goes today.

  • Fewer routine phone calls. Trash day, limb day, when the bill is due, when service is cut off, where a form is — all answered online.
  • A record that the Town responded. A numbered request with a timestamp is the difference between “nobody ever told us” and “the crew closed it on the 17th.”
  • Staff can change the website themselves. Editing a page, posting a notice or correcting a date would no longer require going through anyone outside Town Hall.
  • One announcement, every channel. Posted once, it reaches the website, text, email and the Town’s Facebook page — and the Town keeps its own permanent copy.
  • Fewer disconnection disputes. Publishing cut-off dates in advance, and showing residents their own usage, heads off the calls that come after the fact.

An honest comparison. The ongoing cost is about $480 a year more than the Town pays now — and for that the Town gains a resident portal it does not currently have, plus the ability for staff to make their own changes. For further context, the published rate of the Town’s current platform for a municipality of Decatur’s population is roughly $1,908 a year for the website on its own, so the $720 figure reflects an older arrangement rather than today’s market.
For scale. Against the Town’s audited FY2025 figures, the one-time $2,500 is about 1.4% of general government spending, and $1,200 a year is about 0.7%. The one-time portion is not recurring.

Payment would be half at the start and half at launch, and nothing would be owed until the scope is agreed in writing.

Two reasons the timing matters

A federal accessibility deadline

Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, town websites must meet the WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standard by 26 April 2028 for communities under 50,000. A site built to that standard now avoids paying to correct one later. Helpfully, all 54 of the Town’s ordinance documents already contain real text rather than scanned images, which makes that work substantially cheaper than it might have been.

The Town’s .gov registration

The registration for townofdecaturms.gov carries an expiry date of 3 November 2026. If it were to lapse, the website and every Town email address would stop working at the same moment. This is worth confirming regardless of any decision here — specifically, that a Town employee is a registered domain manager and receives the renewal notices.

Questions the Board may have

Are we locked in?

No. The Town owns the domain, the content and the data, and can request a full export at any time in a standard format. There is no multi-year term and no early-termination penalty.

What happens to everything we run through BBI?

It stays. Nothing moves. The portal would read a balance so residents can see it, and pass anything they submit through to staff to handle as they do now. Billing, administration and records remain in the systems the Town already uses.

Who can change the website?

Town staff. Pages are built from blocks that can be added, removed and reordered. Posting an emergency notice, changing a pickup day or correcting a date takes a minute and requires no outside help.

What is the $100 a month actually for?

Hosting, security updates, keeping it running, and ordinary changes when staff would rather hand something off than do it themselves. Anything substantial — a new section or a new system — would be quoted separately and in advance.

Do residents have to create an account?

No. Paying a bill or reporting a problem would work without signing in. An account is optional and only adds history and reminders.

Is our court information going online?

No. Payment by citation number only. There is no searchable docket and no name lookup. That is a deliberate decision about residents’ privacy.

How long would it take?

Roughly two to three weeks for the public site once the Town’s photographs and documents are gathered, since most of the work already exists. The portal would follow. The Mayor and Clerk review everything before anything goes live.

What if it grows?

The figures reflect a town of roughly 1,900 residents. If Decatur grows substantially, or use runs far beyond expectation, the monthly may need to be revisited — but not without discussing it with the Town first.
