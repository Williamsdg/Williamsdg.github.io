Being straight with you

Things I would not build

Not because they’re hard — because they’d be worse for the Town.

No

Replacing your utility billing

That’s your enterprise fund’s ledger, with statutory receipts, deposits held for years, and audit exposure. Purpose-built packages sell for under $1,500. I’d read from it, never replace it.

No

Taking card payments myself

Keep the hosted processors. The moment card data touches Town systems, you inherit a compliance burden you don’t need.

No

A public court docket

Payment by citation number only. No name search. That’s a deliberate choice about residents’ privacy.

No

A phone app

Birmingham’s 311 app has about a thousand installs for a city of 196,000. A good mobile website serves your 540 households better.

No

Codifying your ordinances

Real work, real value, but it’s a legal product — typically $8,000–$11,000. I’d point you to someone who does it properly.

No

Claiming the website is legal notice

In Mississippi it isn’t. The newspaper still governs. I’d track publication dates and store the affidavits — never pretend to replace them.

Why this list matters. The thing you’re escaping is a vendor who owns pieces of the Town. Everything I build would be in the Town’s name — domain, hosting, content, data — with a full export any time you want it.

How it would work

If you want to move forward

1. Pick the scope

Public site on its own, or with one or both portals. Nothing has to be decided in one sitting.

2. A short written agreement

Plain language: what’s included, what it costs, and that the Town owns the domain, the content and the data, with an export any time.

3. Half up front, half at launch

Nothing is due until you’ve agreed the scope in writing.

4. Content gathering

Photographs, logo, seal, documents, current rates. The heaviest lift on your side, and the one that makes it look like Decatur.

5. Build & review

Roughly two to three weeks for the public site, since most of it already exists. You and your clerk review before anything goes live.

6. Launch & training

Point townofdecaturms.gov at the new site, after domain and mailbox ownership is settled. Then I walk you and your clerk through the editor.

Ask her these:
  • Who at the Town is a registered .gov domain manager, and who gets the renewal notices?
  • Does the Town or the vendor hold the administrator account for the .gov email?
  • Has the Board ever adopted an official code of ordinances, or only the chronological book?
  • Who handles the water billing, and will they produce a nightly export?
  • What does she personally want to change without calling anyone?
Tone: she diagnosed the problem correctly and wrote code to work around it. She’s a peer, not a confused buyer.
