Why this is the piece nobody sells you

Website vendors sell pages. None of them track Mississippi's municipal deadlines, because it is state-specific work that does not scale to fifty states. For a clerk carrying six statutory roles at once, this is the part of the job with real personal exposure — a clerk who signs a warrant for an expenditure not authorized in the minute book commits a misdemeanor under § 21-39-17.

The minute book is still the system of record. Nothing here replaces it. This calendar tells you what has to be entered and when, and keeps the evidence that it was.

Publication tracker

In Mississippi a website is not legal publication. Ordinances, the budget, the tax levy and the audit synopsis take effect through the newspaper. This tracks what was published, when, and stores the publisher's affidavit against it.

Public records requests

Mississippi's Public Records Act sets the clock: respond within seven working days, or up to fourteen if the Town gives written notice of why it needs longer. Denials must be in writing, must cite the specific exemption, and the denial file has to be kept for at least three years.

The record the state expects, not just a file with a name.

Newspaper publication is still required. Under Miss. Code Ann. § 21-13-11 an ordinance does not take effect until it has been published in a newspaper. Posting it on the website is a service to residents — it never substitutes for the newspaper notice, and this system will not let the effective date be set until the publication date is entered.

The accessibility problem, priced

All 54 ordinances are currently PDFs displayed through a third-party viewer. Under the ADA Title II web rule they must meet WCAG 2.1 AA by April 26, 2028. The archive exception does not cover them, because these are the Town's ordinances in force — not historical material kept only for reference.

News & the Facebook problem

The Town's News page is empty today — every announcement lives on Facebook, where it is not searchable, not archived, not a public record the Town controls, and invisible to anyone without an account.

It can also be taken away without warning. In March 2026 Meta disabled thousands of pages in error, with appeals auto-denied within minutes. A town whose only announcement channel is a Facebook page is one wrongful ban away from having no channel at all.

The fix is not "stop using Facebook." Post here once; the system cross-posts to Facebook and keeps the Town's own permanent, searchable copy. Facebook becomes a megaphone instead of the filing cabinet.

Oversight

Compliance watch

The obligations a town of 1,945 people still carries, tracked in one place so they do not become somebody's surprise.

1 · Domain renewal — 42 days. townofdecaturms.gov expires November 3, 2026. A lapse takes down the website and every .gov mailbox at once. Confirm a Town employee is a registered domain manager with their own Login.gov account, and that renewal notices reach Town Hall rather than only the vendor.
2 · Who owns the mailboxes? The Town's mail runs on Google Workspace. If the current vendor holds the administrator account, they control creation, deletion, password resets and the mail itself — and Town email is a public record the Town is legally responsible for. Establish ownership before any other change is made, and never move a DNS record until mailboxes are exported.
3 · Public contact address. The website currently lists decaturpolicems@gmail.com as the Town's contact. The Town already pays for @townofdecaturms.gov addresses. Correspondence sitting in a personal Gmail account is still a public record, and a Gmail address on a .gov site undercuts exactly the trust the .gov is for.

Accessibility — ADA Title II

The Department of Justice rule requires WCAG 2.1 Level AA. For a public entity under 50,000 population the deadline is April 26, 2028, after the one-year extension issued in April 2026.
