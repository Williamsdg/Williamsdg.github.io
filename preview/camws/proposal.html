The conceptwilliamsdigital.io/preview/camws/

We built it first.

A working concept for CAMWS: a homepage, a member portal, and meeting registration with abstract submission. It uses CAMWS's own published content, dues, and dates. It is unlisted and hidden from search engines.

What the concept demonstrates

  • A membership finder. A visitor chooses "student" or "K-12 teacher" and sees the dues and what membership offers them.
  • An awards finder. CAMWS awards filtered by who may apply, each with its deadline.
  • A live "Coming up" list drawn from the dates CAMWS has published.
  • Join or renew in one step, with journal format and a designated gift.
  • Meeting registration and abstract submission that begin with an automatic membership check.
  • A staff view with membership reports and spreadsheet export.

Member names and staff figures in the concept are invented samples and are labeled as such. Dues, dates, awards, and descriptions are CAMWS's own.

Section 1 · Executive summaryWilliams Digital

Executive summary

The RFP says a modernized website is "a prerequisite for the broader work of organizational renewal." We agree, and we have read the current site with that in mind. The problem is less how it looks than what a graduate student or a first-year teacher has to do to join, renew, register, or find a deadline.

What we found on the current site

  • Joining takes several steps and a wait. A new member fills in a form, then pays through PayPal with a $3 handling fee or prints the form and mails a check. The site notes that access to the Loeb Classical Library can take two weeks to arrive.
  • Member benefits sit behind one shared password. The Loeb, JPASS, Oxford University Press, and GreekKeys pages are password protected, and members email the office to ask for the password. There is no member sign-in.
  • Deadlines are spread across pages. Abstract deadlines, the hotel block, and more than twenty award deadlines each live on their own page, in paragraphs.
  • The site is large. Its site map lists about 1,490 pages, roughly half of them numbered pages with no descriptive address, alongside older sections built before the current system.
  • The platform has reached end of life. The site runs on Drupal 7, which stopped receiving security updates from its maintainers in January 2025.
  • Phones cannot zoom. The page template turns off pinch-to-zoom, which fails a WCAG requirement before any other testing begins.

What we propose

  • A WordPress website that CAMWS owns outright, with a small custom theme and no page builder. Staff edit labeled fields ("Deadline," "Award name," "Meeting hotel"), and the layout holds.
  • WildApricot as the membership system of record, as the RFP suggests: applications, renewals, automatic reminders, the directory, dues payments, and staff reports. Members sign in once, and that sign-in opens the benefit pages.
  • Meeting registration and abstract submission tied to membership, so a member never retypes a name, and the Program Committee reviews abstracts without seeing who wrote them.
  • One calendar that feeds the homepage, the meeting page, and the awards pages, so a date is entered once.
  • A migration that decides, page by page, what to keep, rewrite, or retire, with redirects so existing links and citations keep working.

Why Williams Digital

Williams Digital is a small studio in Birmingham, Alabama, inside the CAMWS region. Dylan Williams designs and builds every site and stays on as the support contact after launch. Our closest comparable work is a community association website whose staff post their own events, newsletters, and documents; a membership-style directory with online applications, payments, and an approval dashboard; and a media platform that nontechnical staff publish to every day.

We would rather show than describe, so we built the concept before writing this proposal.

Cost and schedule in brief. $34,500 fixed for the full scope in the RFP, itemized by phase in section 9. Recurring platform and hosting costs are listed separately and would be paid by CAMWS directly to each provider. Launch is planned for early June 2027, before the membership year begins on July 1.

What we want to be direct about

We have not yet built a site for a scholarly association, and the sites we have delivered run on content systems we built ourselves. We recommend WordPress and WildApricot for CAMWS anyway, because the RFP asks for a platform that does not depend on one vendor's specialized knowledge, and that includes ours. Section 7 says more about this and about how a one-person studio protects CAMWS from depending on one person.

Section 2 · Technical approach · Platform and componentsWilliams Digital

Technical approach

Two well-supported systems, each doing the job it is best at, joined by a single member sign-in.

Platform recommendation and rationale

  • WordPress for the website and content. It is open source, has no license fee, and is the most widely used content management system, so CAMWS can find help anywhere if it ever changes vendors. Editing, scheduling, user roles, and version history with rollback are built in.
  • WildApricot for membership, dues, and meeting registration. It is built for associations of CAMWS's size and replaces manual records with a single member database. It handles renewals and reminders, online payments, a member directory, event registration, and financial reports without custom software to maintain.
  • Why not one system for everything. Membership plugins inside WordPress avoid a monthly fee but add software that must be updated and tested by a developer. WildApricot's own website builder would be simpler still, but it would not meet the RFP's requirements for structured content, design control, and data portability. We would review this choice with the committee at kickoff before anything is built.

Section 3 · Technical approach · Technical requirementsWilliams Digital

Accessibility (RFP V.A)

The site would be built to WCAG 2.2 Level AA from the first design, not corrected at the end.

  • Testing methodology. Automated scans of every template during the build. Manual keyboard testing of every interactive element. Screen reader testing with VoiceOver and NVDA on the join, renew, register, and submit journeys. Contrast measured on every color pair. Zoom to 200% and 400% with no loss of content.
  • Remediation. Each finding is logged with its WCAG criterion, fixed, and retested. CAMWS receives the written report before launch.
  • Keeping it accessible. Editors work in fields, so headings, contrast, and structure come from the template. An image cannot be published without a text alternative. Uploaded PDFs are the main risk, and the training covers how to prepare them.

Security and compliance (RFP IV.H and V.E)

  • Patching. WordPress core and plugin updates are applied within one week of release, and critical security releases within 48 hours, on a staging copy first.
  • Few moving parts. A custom theme and a short list of maintained plugins, each named in the handover document with its purpose.
  • Access. Multi-factor authentication is required for every administrative account. Roles are limited to what each person needs, and an activity log records who changed what and when.
  • Protection. Managed hosting with a firewall, malware scanning, and HTTPS throughout.
  • Backups and recovery. Automatic daily backups kept for 30 days, a restore test before launch, and a written recovery procedure.

Data ownership and portability (RFP V.D)

CAMWS owns every account, the domain, the theme and its source files, all content, and all member data. Hosting, WildApricot, email, and analytics are opened in CAMWS's name with CAMWS's billing. Website content exports in WordPress's standard format. Member, payment, and registration records export from WildApricot as spreadsheets at any time, and we would set up a scheduled export to CAMWS's own storage so a current copy always exists outside the platform.

Search and discoverability (RFP V.F)

  • Site search. Search across pages, news, awards, and the document library, with results grouped by type.
  • Search engines and AI-assisted search. Clean page titles and descriptions, descriptive addresses in place of numbered ones, an automatic site map, and schema.org structured data for the organization, events, and articles, so meeting dates and deadlines can be read by machines as well as people.
  • Semantic markup. Real headings, lists, tables, and landmarks, which serve screen readers and indexing services alike.

Performance (RFP V.G)

The target is public pages that load in under two seconds on a typical phone connection, inside the RFP's three-second limit. The theme is hand-coded with no page builder. Pages are cached and served through a content delivery network. Images are compressed and sized automatically on upload. We test before launch and report the results.

Future sustainability (RFP V.H)

  • Maintenance burden. About two to three hours a month: updates, backup checks, and monitoring. Staff content editing needs no developer.
  • Recurring licensing costs. WildApricot, hosting, and one forms license. All are listed with estimates in section 9.
  • Five-year upgrade path. WordPress releases updates several times a year and keeps older sites working. The custom theme uses only standard WordPress features, so no rebuild is expected within five years. WildApricot is updated by its provider at no cost to CAMWS.
  • Reliance on specialized expertise. None beyond ordinary WordPress knowledge. Any WordPress developer could maintain this site from the handover document.

Content migration plan

  1. Inventory. We crawl the current site and list every page, file, and form, with its traffic and last edit. The site map lists about 1,490 pages. We expect a much smaller number to be in active use.
  2. Decide. Staff review the list with us, and each item is marked keep, rewrite, combine, or retire. Archives with lasting value, such as past meetings, committee charters, necrologies, and newsletters, are kept and organized. Nothing is deleted without approval.
  3. Move. Structured content (awards, committees, meetings, officers) is entered into its new fields. Long-form pages and PDFs are transferred with their text checked. Documents go into the library.
  4. Members. Current membership records are cleaned with the home office and imported into WildApricot. Each member receives an email to set a password. No one is asked to rejoin.
  5. Redirect. Every old address is mapped to its new page, including the older addresses still linked from journals, syllabi, and other societies' sites.
  6. Check. Quality assurance runs during the move and again after launch: broken-link scans, a redirect test of every old address, and a review of the most-visited pages by staff.

The Classical Journal, Teaching Classical Languages, and Southern Section websites are separate sites today. They are not included in the migration unless CAMWS asks. See the questions in section 10.

Section 6 · Training, launch, and ongoing supportWilliams Digital

Training and support

Training and documentation

  • Two live sessions for the home office, recorded: managing content, and managing members, renewals, and meeting registration.
  • One session for officers, editors, and committee chairs who will edit their own sections.
  • A written user guide with screenshots, organized by task: post an announcement, add a deadline, replace a document, open a call for papers, record a check, run a renewal report.
  • Short video tutorials, two to four minutes each, for the tasks staff do most.
  • A handover document listing every account, plugin, setting, and procedure, written so another developer could take over without calling us.

Launch and post-launch

Before launch we test every template on phones, tablets, and desktops, run the full accessibility and performance tests, restore a backup, and run a real test payment and refund. For 60 days after launch we monitor the site daily, fix anything that surfaces at no charge, and review search and traffic reports with staff. At day 60 CAMWS receives the handover document and chooses whether to continue with a support plan.

A one-person studio, and how CAMWS is protected

The RFP asks for key personnel who are available throughout, and for a solution that does not rely on specialized expertise. A one-person team is a real consideration, and we would rather name it than hide it. It shaped this proposal in four ways.

  • Standard platforms. WordPress and WildApricot are widely used and well documented. Neither depends on us.
  • Everything in CAMWS's name. Hosting, the domain, WildApricot, email, and analytics belong to CAMWS from the first day.
  • Source files and documentation. The theme's source is kept in a repository CAMWS owns, and the handover document is written for a developer who has never met us.
  • No required retainer. The support plan is optional and can be cancelled at any time.

Experience with the proposed platform

The RFP asks for proven experience with the proposed content management system. The client sites in our portfolio run on staff content systems that we designed and built ourselves, because that suited each client. A WordPress theme is made of the same HTML, CSS, and JavaScript we write every day, and our hand-coded designs move into one without a page builder. A WildApricot integration at CAMWS's scale would be new for us. We would set up a working WildApricot trial and sign-in on the staging site in the first weeks of the project, so the committee can judge it early.

Why this project

Dylan works full time in ministry, so he knows small-staff organizations from the inside: board approvals, volunteers, and websites kept up by people with other jobs to do. CAMWS met in Mobile in 2026 at the invitation of the Alabama Classical Association, and Samford University, here in Birmingham, is an institutional member. We would be glad to build this for an association with roots in our own state.
