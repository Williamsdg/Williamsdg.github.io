1936 — 1988

52 Years of Service

From Depression-era shipyards to the Florida Straits — keep scrolling to sail through five decades of INGHAM's story.

Scroll to sail
1936
Commissioned

USCGC INGHAM (WHEC-35) enters service on September 12, 1936 — one of seven 327-foot Secretary-class cutters built for the United States Coast Guard.

1942
World War II

Neutrality patrols, weather stations, and North Atlantic convoy escort. On December 17, 1942, INGHAM is credited with sinking the German submarine U-626.

1968
Vietnam

Deployed to Operation Market Time in July 1968 — interdiction patrols, naval gunfire support, and rescue operations. INGHAM's crews earn two Presidential Unit Citations, unmatched by any other Coast Guard cutter.

1980
Mariel Boatlift

As more than one hundred thousand Cubans cross the Florida Straits, INGHAM rescues refugees from overloaded vessels and escorts them safely into Key West.

1988
Decommissioned

After 52 years, INGHAM retires on May 27, 1988 as the most decorated vessel in the Coast Guard fleet — and is designated a National Historic Landmark.

2009
Home in Key West

INGHAM arrives at Truman Waterfront as the centerpiece of her memorial museum — preserved by volunteers and funded entirely by the people who step aboard.

A Living MemorialTo Those Who Never Came Home

INGHAM is more than a museum ship. She stands as a memorial to the Coast Guardsmen who gave their lives in World War II and Vietnam — a place where their names are honored aboard a ship that shared their service.

In honored memory of the Coast Guardsmen lost in the Second World War and in the waters of Vietnam
