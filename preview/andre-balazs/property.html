Chiltern Firehouse

A reborn 19th-century firehouse — twenty-six rooms, a Nuno Mendes restaurant, and a courtyard that closes at no particular time.

"The most charismatic hotel to open in London in a generation."

— Vogue

i. The Story
Chapter one

A red-brick firehouse, hiding in plain sight.

The Chiltern Street firehouse was built in 1889 — for ninety years it housed horses, then engines, then nothing in particular. By 2010 it was a Grade II-listed shell with a beautiful tower and a forgotten courtyard.

It took three years and a small army of craftsmen to bring it back. The bones were preserved. The rest was invented.

Chapter two

A kitchen at the heart of it.

Chef Nuno Mendes was given the keys before the wallpaper went up. The result is a restaurant that feels like the building's reason for existing — wood-fired, ingredient-led, and nearly impossible to book.

The crab donuts have their own Wikipedia entry. The dining room has its own gravity.

Chapter three

Twenty-six rooms, each its own.

No two rooms are the same. A loft suite in the old tower. A garden room with French doors onto the courtyard. Ladder Suites with library walls. We do not believe in standardization — only in getting each room exactly right.
