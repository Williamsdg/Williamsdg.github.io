Website Proposal · Byron's Smokehouse

You don't need a new website. You need the Byron's experience, online.

Byron's already has the thing most restaurants spend decades trying to build: history. Thirty-five years on Opelika Road, generations of Auburn customers, and a reputation that reaches well past Lee County. Right now the website doesn't tell any of it.

Where things stand

The current site works like a business card.

Everything on it is accurate. That isn't the problem. The problem is that it stops at the facts.

What's there now

  • Address, phone number, and hours
  • A handful of photos
  • Social links and a contact form
  • Delivery pointed to DoorDash
  • A mention that catering is available

What's missing

  • Any version of the Byron's story — 1989, the pit, the regulars
  • A menu people can actually read on a phone
  • Breakfast treated as its own draw, not a footnote
  • Game-day catering positioned where visitors will find it
  • The national press coverage Byron's has already earned
  • Anything that answers “where should we eat in Auburn?”
The idea

Build the smokehouse, not a restaurant template.

More than barbecue. It's an Auburn tradition.

The site should feel like walking into a place that's been collecting stories since 1989 — smoke, wood, butcher paper, old photographs, hand-painted signage, biscuits at six in the morning, barbecue before kickoff.

Not polished corporate BBQ. Not a trendy restaurant theme that any of a thousand places could be using. Something that could only be Byron's, because the history behind it belongs to Byron's alone.

That positioning is also the part competitors can't copy. Anybody can open a barbecue restaurant. Nobody else has been on Opelika Road since 1989.

Visual direction

Smoke, paper, wood, fire.

Deliberately not orange-and-blue everywhere — that reads cheap fast. Auburn shows up as a quiet navy and an ember orange that's really about the pit.

Typography

  • Slab-ish serif headlines — old Southern newspaper, not cowboy
  • Condensed uppercase for stamps, labels, and signage
  • Clean sans for anything someone has to actually read

Texture

  • Weathered wood and butcher paper
  • Newsprint grain over photography
  • Drifting smoke behind the hero type
  • Slightly desaturated, warm photo treatment
How it runs

Four phases, no mystery.

Phase 01

Story & Archive

Sit down and pull the history together — the 1989 story, old photographs, clippings, menus, anything in a box in the back. This is the raw material the whole site is built from, and it's the part only Byron's can supply.

Phase 02

Photography

A shoot covering the pit, the biscuits, the plates, the room, and the morning crowd. Good food photography is most of what separates this from the current site.

Phase 03

Build

Full site build on the concept you're looking at — mobile-first, fast, and set up so Byron's can update hours, menu, and specials without calling anybody.

Phase 04

Found & Maintained

Local search setup so Byron's turns up for “Auburn barbecue” and “breakfast near Auburn University,” plus ongoing updates through football season.
