One Sunday, Twenty-Six Campuses, One Record

Right now every campus debriefs a weekend somewhere different — a text thread, a notes doc, a conversation in the hallway on Monday. The knowledge exists, but it isn't comparable, searchable, or carried forward to next year. Highlands Ops takes the After Action Report model used by the military and hospital systems and shapes it around a weekend service.

📋 Capture

Every campus fills out the same structured report after every event — attendance, setup, production, facilities, weather, wins, misses.

🔭 Compare

Because all 26 use the same fields, Central Ops can see the whole network at once instead of reading 26 different documents.

↻ Carry Forward

Layouts, playbooks and action items persist year over year, so next Christmas starts from last Christmas instead of from scratch.

The design principle: the thing people do 90% of the time — open this Sunday and fill out my campus's report — is three clicks from launch. Everything else lives one level deeper.

The Core Screen

The Campus Report

One structured form per campus per event. It's long on purpose — but it's the same form every time, so filling it out becomes muscle memory and the data stays comparable across all 26 locations.

📊 Service Snapshot

Attendance that auto-totals, plus guests, salvations, setup times and weather — with a follow-up question when weather actually affected operations.

⭐ Experience Scorecard

Nine rated areas from parking to accessibility. Rate anything three stars or below and the report immediately asks why — a star with no context isn't actionable.

🛰 Visual Layouts

Exterior, lobby and seating layouts sit inside the report itself, not buried under an uploads tab. How the building was actually configured.

🔧 Operations Health

Ten systems from audio to HVAC, shown as a compact grid. A detail box only opens when something is actually wrong.

👥 Key Positions

Who was leading each area. Volunteer scheduling comes from Planning Center; this records what actually happened.

🟢 Wins & Opportunities

Two columns, categorized and rated for impact. Every opportunity has a one-click button to create an action item.

🏆 Recognition

Name a volunteer or staff member in the moment, with a category. It flows to the network Recognition wall instead of being forgotten by Tuesday.

📷 Documentation

Photos organized by operational area — exterior, lobby, auditorium, parking — rather than by file type. Far more useful a year later.

The detail that matters most: action items are created throughout the report, wherever a problem surfaces — and they arrive prefilled with the campus, event, category and issue. Nobody retypes anything, and no lesson depends on someone remembering it at the bottom of a long form.
Two buttons worth clicking at the top of the report: 📐 Blueprint opens that campus's permanent profile — floor plans, AED locations, utility shutoffs, camera positions — the reference every event points back to. ✦ AI Summary drafts the executive summary of the report so a leader can read the weekend in fifteen seconds instead of five minutes.
The Differentiator

Visual Operations

Most reporting tools capture words and numbers. Church operations are physical — where the coffee went, how the parking flowed, what the stage looked like. This section captures the layout itself.

🛰 Exterior

Satellite view for parking, tents, traffic routes and shuttle pickup.

🏢 Lobby

Floor plan with coffee, Connect Center, photo booth, prayer and merch placed on it.

💺 Seating

Reserved, VIP, overflow, wheelchair, camera positions and prayer team.

🎤 Stage

Stage plot — band, drums, keys, cameras, lighting and speaker positions.

🚗 Parking Plan

Entrance, exit, overflow, shuttle, police posts and first aid.

📷 Photo Galleries

Setup, service and teardown photos filed by area — exterior, lobby, auditorium, kids, backstage, signage.

Then the part that pays for itself: every layout keeps a version history, and AI Compare to Last Year puts this year's layout beside last year's and describes what changed — "coffee moved to the north wall, 214 seats added." That's the institutional memory that normally walks out the door when a staff member leaves.
Try it: Event workspace → Visual Operations → open any layout card, then click Compare to Last Year.
Reference

Two Levels, Four Tabs Each

The whole system is two layers deep. Central Operations works at the event level; a campus works inside its own report. Four tabs each — the structure never grows as years of events accumulate.

Event levelWhat it's for
CampusesThe default landing tab. Status header with clickable filters, plus all 26 campus cards showing attendance, issues, actions and recognition.
SummaryThe aggregated After Action Report — network totals, plus what went well and what didn't, tallied across every campus so nobody reads 26 reports.
VisualsEvery campus's layouts side by side, so Central can see how 26 campuses executed the same event.
Action ItemsOnly the follow-ups this event generated.
Campus levelWhat it's for
ReportThe After Action Report itself — what happened.
VisualsThis campus's exterior, lobby, seating and parking layouts, with version history.
PlaybookWhat to do next time. Findings get promoted from the report into permanent instructions.
HistoryThe same campus over time, with two occurrences comparable side by side.
For Leadership

The Central Ops View

The sidebar has a "Viewing As" switcher. Set it to Central to see the whole network, or to any single campus to see exactly what that campus's team sees. Two pages exist only at this level.

✦ Insights

Opens on findings, not a blank chat box — recurring issues, emerging trends, and what's working across the network. Then Ask Intelligence answers plain-language questions with structured results and a Sources list linking to the actual reports behind every conclusion. AI finds the pattern; leadership makes the call.

✅ Action Items & 🏆 Recognition

Two network-wide rolls: every open task with owner, campus, due date and priority; and every volunteer or staff member named across all 26 campuses. Both are filterable and both are visible to leadership without asking anyone for a status update.

Try it: Switch "Viewing As" from Central to Auburn and watch the whole interface narrow to one campus. Same system, different altitude.
Where It Gets Used

Built for a Phone in a Lobby

Almost nobody fills out an after-action report at a desk. They do it standing in a lobby at 12:40 on a Sunday, on a phone, before the details fade. The demo is fully responsive — open it on your phone and you'll get a proper mobile app layout: a slide-out menu, a campus picker in the top bar, and a five-item bottom tab bar for the screens people use most.

Worth doing: pull this page up on your phone and open the demo there. It's the single fastest way to understand whether the reporting habit would actually stick across 26 campuses.
Straight Answer

What's Real and What's Simulated

This is a working concept demo, and it's worth being precise about which parts are which.

✅ Real in the demo

Every screen, tab, form, filter and navigation path is genuinely built and clickable. The 26 campuses are Highlands' actual locations. Your edits to action item statuses persist in your browser while you explore.

⚠️ Simulated for now

All attendance, notes and recognition data is realistic but invented. Planning Center, ProPresenter, live weather and the AI features are mocked to show the interaction — in a production build they connect to real services and a real AI model.

What a production build looks like: the same interface on a real application framework with a secure database, live Planning Center and ProPresenter integration, real weather data, genuine AI summarization, role-based permissions per campus, and single sign-on for staff.
