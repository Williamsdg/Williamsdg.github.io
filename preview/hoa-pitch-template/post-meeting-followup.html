After the Board Meeting

Three follow-up scenarios with pre-written emails and timing. Send the right one within 24 hours of the meeting — this is when momentum is highest.

Scenario A — They Voted YES
Send within 12 hours of the meeting · 8am next morning is ideal
Email 1 — Same evening or next morning
To: Margaret Chen <mchen@briarwoodca.com>
Cc: [Board Chair email]
Subj: Thank you — kickoff next steps for Briarwood

Jennifer (and board) —

Thank you for the trust. Last night meant a lot, and I'm excited to get to work.

Here's what's coming in the next 7 days:

  • Today: I'm sending the service agreement via DocuSign — it has all the terms we walked through (pricing schedule, SLA, offboarding clauses, 24-month flagship rate).
  • This week: Once signed, I'll send a kickoff calendar invite for next week (90 min) and a short pre-kickoff worksheet so we hit the ground running.
  • Week 1: Workflow audit, brand asset handoff, and the authorization form for me to request the data export from your current vendor.

If anything comes up between now and the agreement landing, my number is 205-XXX-XXXX. Direct line, anytime.

Looking forward to building something Briarwood is proud of.

— Dylan
Williams Digital

Email 2 — 24 hours later (after sending agreement)
To: Margaret Chen
Subj: Quick checklist for our kickoff meeting

Jennifer —

Quick prep so the kickoff is the most useful 90 minutes possible. By next Tuesday, would you be able to gather:

  • Logo files (vector preferred — .ai, .svg, .pdf)
  • Most recent CC&Rs and bylaws PDFs
  • List of Briarwood-approved photographers (we'll plan a shoot for the public site after launch)
  • Names + emails of anyone the board wants to be CC'd on weekly demos
  • Your current management vendor's customer-success contact (so I can request the data export myself once you authorize it)

None of these are blockers — we can start without any of it. But the more we have day one, the faster the design phase moves.

— Dylan

What's next: By end of week 1, you should have a signed agreement, a kickoff meeting on the calendar, and the data-export request out to their current vendor. Don't let the ball drop here — week 1 momentum sets the tone for the whole 8 weeks.
Scenario B — They TABLED the Decision
Send within 24 hours · Don't push, but don't disappear
Email 1 — The day after the meeting
To: Margaret Chen
Cc: [Board Chair]
Subj: Thank you — and what would help the board decide?

Jennifer (and board) —

Thank you for the time and the thoughtful questions last night. I respect that this is a real decision and you want to make sure it's the right one for Briarwood.

I want to be useful between now and your next meeting. A few things I can do:

  • Answer questions in writing. If anything came up that I didn't fully answer (or you want a written record of), I'll send detailed responses by [3 days from now].
  • Connect you with another HOA decision-maker. If a reference call would help, I can get one set up — we have other prospects in similar shoes who've been through this evaluation.
  • Hold pricing. The Flagship rate ($500/mo, 24-month lock) is yours through Q3 — no pressure on timing.

One specific ask: what would a "yes" need to look like? If it's specific information, I'll provide it. If it's more time to socialize internally, I'll give you space. Either is fine — I'd rather do this right than fast.

— Dylan

Email 2 — Day 7 (only if no reply yet)
To: Margaret Chen
Subj: Check-in on a few small things

Jennifer —

Just floating this back to the top — no urgency. Two small updates that might be useful while the board considers things:

  • I noticed three of last week's questions came up in another HOA conversation, so I wrote up clear written answers (attached). Feel free to forward to anyone on the board who'd find them useful.
  • If anyone wants to take another lap through the live demo, the link is still active: williamsdigital.io/hoa-pitch-template/pitch.html

Whenever the board's ready to move, I'm here. And if the answer ends up being "not now," I'd genuinely appreciate a brief note on what would have made it a yes — that helps me serve the next association better.

— Dylan

Don't: Send more than 2 follow-ups in 30 days. After that, leave it alone for 60–90 days, then a single "checking in" message. Board decisions on capex move at board pace, not vendor pace.
Scenario C — They Voted NO
Send within 24 hours · Be gracious, learn from it
Email 1 — The day after
To: Margaret Chen
Cc: [Board Chair]
Subj: Thank you — and an honest ask

Jennifer (and board) —

Thank you for the time and the candid conversation last night. I respect the decision and I'm grateful you let me make my case to the full board.

One ask, if you're willing: what would have changed your mind?

I'm not asking to reopen the conversation — your decision is your decision. But the honest answer would help me serve the next association better. Was it pricing? Timeline? Risk of switching vendors? Something I didn't address well? I take feedback seriously and I'd rather hear the truth than a polite "it just wasn't the right fit."

If the answer is none of the above and the timing simply isn't right, that's also useful to know. The Flagship rate I quoted is held for Briarwood through Q3 — if circumstances change, the door's open.

Either way, I wish Briarwood well. Best of luck with whichever direction you go.

— Dylan
Williams Digital

Email 2 — 90 days later (only if no negative feedback received)
To: Margaret Chen
Subj: How's it going?

Jennifer —

Hope you're well. Curious how things are going at Briarwood. No agenda — just thinking back to our conversation in May and wondering whether the path you chose is working out the way you hoped.

If everything's great, that's wonderful and I genuinely mean it. If anything has come up where I could be useful, the door's still open.

Either way, all the best to you and the team.

— Dylan

Why send Email 2: Most "no" decisions get reconsidered within 6–18 months when the chosen platform's per-door costs creep up or the vendor proves unresponsive. The 90-day check-in keeps you top-of-mind without being a pest. Roughly 1 in 5 cold "no"s become a "yes" after the 90-day touch.

Pipeline Building

While Briarwood Decides, Line Up the Next 5 HOAs

Whether Briarwood signs or not, the small-HOA pitch template is ready. Use this targeting framework to find 5 prospects to pitch within 30 days of May 11.

Tier 1 · Flagship-Tier (300+ homes)

  • Master-planned communities in Birmingham metro
  • Hoover, Hoover, Mountain Brook, Homewood, Trussville
  • Currently using AppFolio / Buildium / FrontSteps
  • $500/mo plan, 24-month flagship rate

Tier 2 · Standard (100–300 homes)

  • Single-neighborhood HOAs with their own board
  • Currently using PayHOA, HOA Office, or self-managing
  • Outdated WordPress sites, no portal
  • $179/mo Standard tier

Tier 3 · Starter (under 100 homes)

  • Small subdivisions, condo associations, townhouse communities
  • Often self-managing, no platform at all
  • Most price-sensitive, fastest to close
  • $99/mo Starter tier

Hi [Director name] — I help HOAs replace per-door SaaS platforms (AppFolio, Buildium, PayHOA) with custom portals that don't scale against you as you grow. Pipeline Cadence

Week 1 (post-May 11): Identify 15 Tier-1/2/3 prospects.
Week 2: First-touch email to all 8 (10 min each = 2.5 hr).
Week 3: Follow up with non-responders. Schedule first 3 demo calls.
Week 4: Send pitch to interested parties. Aim: 5 active conversations by Day 30.
