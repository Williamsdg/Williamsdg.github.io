    Two places under one roof

    A warm, planted greenhouse where butterflies fly free, and an exhibit hall of live insects, spiders and their relatives.

    The Butterfly House

    A tropical greenhouse filled with butterflies and moths from around the world. They share it with quail, a few finches and Tina, the pacman frog. Stay on the paths and take your time.

    Butterfly release, Tuesday to Saturday at 11 a.m. and Sunday at 12:30 p.m.

    Watch newly emerged butterflies be released into the greenhouse. You'll likely see something new every time. Included with admission.

    The Insectarium

    An exhibit area with a wide array of insects, arachnids, millipedes, centipedes and crustaceans.

    Ever wonder how a tarantula eats? Come for predator feeding at 3:30 p.m., Tuesday to Saturday. There's a different predator every week, so check in and see who's hungry.

    What's buzzin' in Montana

    Every summer week the Butterfly House shares the insects and spiders being spotted around the state. The season wrapped on September 25, and the series is back when things start buzzing again.

    Read past weeks

    September 25, 2026

    Knapweed root weevil

    A small grey weevil introduced to fight invasive spotted knapweed. Its larvae tunnel into the roots. Photo: Gabrielle Weller.

    September 18, 2026

    Shamrock orb weaver

    Builds a large, circular web at night, then waits in a sheltered spot nearby during the day. Photo: Kathleen Ort.

    September 11, 2026

    Polyphemus moth caterpillar

    Grows to 3 to 4 inches and eats 86,000 times its own weight in leaves before it pupates. Photo: Deb Hoagland.

    Keep the butterflies flying

    The Butterfly House is a nonprofit, and the butterflies don't come free.

    Aside from staff salaries, keeping the butterfly house filled with beautiful butterflies from around the world is our second largest annual expense.

    Monthly gifts from the Flight Crew go directly to buying butterflies.
