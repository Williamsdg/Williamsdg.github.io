Apollo Jewelry · Mobile

A jewellery store
that fits in a hand

Most people who buy from Apollo Jewelry meet it twice: once on Ipapantis, and once afterwards — on a phone, from somewhere else in the world. This is that second meeting, designed properly: large imagery, one-thumb browsing, simple filters, and a short path from a piece to a purchase.

The screens Browsing & filters Product detail Sizing, bag & checkout ● Live, scrollable preview Optional app concept

01 — Arrival

The first ten seconds

A full-bleed piece of the house's own photography, the name, and one decision to make. Nothing to dismiss, no pop-up, no carousel of offers.

01One thumbEvery primary action — filter, save, open, add to bag — sits in the lower two-thirds of the screen.
02Image firstProduct photography runs edge to edge. Nothing is cropped into a rounded card or boxed in a shadow.
03Honest spec14k or 18k, the stones, and the length in centimetres. The information a jewellery buyer actually checks.
04FastImages are re-encoded to WebP and lazy-loaded — grid thumbnails average 17 KB against the multi-megabyte originals.

02 — Deciding, and buying

The short path
from piece to purchase

All sales are final and exchanges happen in person in Fira — so the mobile design puts sizing, shipping and payment in front of the customer before the bag, not after it.

03 — Not a mockup

Scroll the real thing

The frame beside this is the concept site itself, running at 390 px — the same page as the desktop version, the same 149 pieces, the same prices. Scroll it, filter it, open a piece, add it to the bag.

Everything in the mobile design is the site responding to the screen it is on. There is no separate mobile site to maintain, and no piece of the catalogue that is desktop-only.

And if there were
an app

Apollo Jewelry does not currently have a mobile app, and nothing in this concept assumes one. But the house sells to two kinds of customer an app suits particularly well: the visitor who sees a piece on Ipapantis and flies home the next morning, and the returning customer buying again from the other side of the world.

Could holdPrivate previewsNew pieces shown to returning customers before they reach the shop floor.
Could holdWish lists that follow youSaved across devices and shareable — the way fine jewellery is actually bought.
Could holdSizing & exclusive releasesSaved sizes, a direct line to the family, and limited workshop pieces offered to app customers first.
To be clearNone of this existsNo Apollo Jewelry app exists today. This is an illustration of what one could do, not a claim or a commitment.

Apollo Jewelry — a family jewellery house on Ipapantis Street, Fira, Santorini. Founded 1985 by Yiannis Psomas and Karin Steiner, continued today with their daughter Alexandra.
