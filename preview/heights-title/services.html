What a title search looks for

We carefully scrutinize government records of property ownership to make sure everything is legitimate and there are no nasty surprises lurking in the property’s past. If any of these are hiding in the documentation, you need to know before you take ownership.

  • Unpaid liens
  • Easement issues
  • Any limitations on future development
  • Contested property ownership claims

Title insurance looks backward

Homeowner’s and auto insurance protect you from things that may happen in the future. Title insurance protects you from things that may have happened in the past. If an unknown heir to the property turns up years down the road, title insurance covers the cost of defending your rights in court.

We issue a policy to you as the owner, and to your lender, which protects their stake for the life of the loan.

If you’re selling

As the seller, you’ll have fewer responsibilities in the transaction than the buyer, but there are expenses you need to be aware of.

Usually it’s the buyer who chooses the title company and starts the process. Your closing costs will likely come out of the home sale proceeds, but it’s still a good idea to anticipate them.

Refinance

Need cash or a lower interest rate? Refinancing may be your best option.

Refinancing simply means trading in your old loan for a new one. Most often borrowers choose to refinance to take advantage of a lower interest rate and reduce their monthly payments.

The benefits of refinancing

  • Use the equity in your home for expenses like college, a car or boat, or medical bills
  • Reduce your monthly payment with a lower interest rate
  • Save money on total interest paid
  • Pay off your mortgage faster

Know your refinancing options

Loan term

Lenders generally offer 10, 15, 20, or 30 year mortgages. What is best for you in the short term and the long term?

Interest rate

Rates rise and fall due to a variety of economic factors. Refinancing with a lower interest rate may reduce your monthly payments.

Cash out

A cash out refinance lets you turn the equity in your home into cash you can spend. What are your financial needs and goals?

How to prepare

Partner with a trusted lender and find out the details you need to move forward:

  • Does your credit score qualify you for lending?
  • Do you have equity in your home? If so, how much?
  • What are the terms and details of your current mortgage?

If I’m refinancing, do I need a new title insurance policy?

Well, yes and no. Your owner’s policy remains until you no longer own the home, so you won’t need a new one. But the lender’s policy only lasts as long as the current loan terms. You’ll need another lender’s policy if you refinance with new terms or another institution.

Questions we hear often

How long does a title search take?

The age of the house is the biggest factor. A longer ownership history means more records to investigate. As a rule of thumb, set aside 10–14 days for a complete title search.

What does a title company actually do?

Three main things: find and eliminate any issues that may affect your rights to ownership, provide title insurance for the lender and borrower, and handle closing procedures and closing escrow accounts.

Who chooses the title company?

Usually it’s the buyer, as they are the ones paying for the bulk of it. They’ll also contact the title company and start the process. Your real estate agent can recommend one.

How are title companies paid?

Through closing costs. Several factors go into how much, like the property’s price and the state it’s located in. The closing attorney and your agent can review an itemized list of expenses at closing.

Do I need a survey?

A survey may be required to show the legal property boundaries, and it is a good idea to have one done either way. It confirms you are getting all of the property you are paying for, and that nobody encroaches on it.

What happens if the search finds a problem?

Things like a tax lien or a judgment do come up. Part of our role is to make sure everything is cleared ahead of time so nothing comes up at the closing table. Read: Title search problems, what happens next
