The reality of cloud signage

Your screens shouldn't go dark because somewhere else went down.

Every other major signage platform — ScreenCloud, Yodeck, OptiSigns, Rise Vision — streams from the cloud. That means a flaky router, an ISP outage, or a vendor blip turns your menu boards, lobby loops, and announcement screens into black rectangles. At the worst possible moment.

SUNDAY MORNING

Church Wi-Fi can't handle the crowd.

Lobby screens freeze, kids check-in loops stall, and the welcome graphics never load. Right when a thousand people walk in.

FRIDAY DINNER RUSH

Menu boards blank out mid-shift.

The cloud signage subscription was working fine — until Comcast had a regional outage. Customers can't see the menu. Cashiers can't take orders. Pencil and paper time.

EVERY MORNING

School announcement screens spin forever.

Bandwidth is throttled, the cloud player times out, and the daily bulletin never appears. Teachers improvise with a printed sheet.

How Anchor works

One hub. Every screen. No cloud dependency.

The Anchor Hub runs on a machine you already own — a Mac mini, a small PC, even a Raspberry Pi. It stores your media locally and broadcasts to every screen on your network. Screens cache their content so they keep playing, no matter what.

Common questions

What everyone asks first.

What hardware do I actually need?

One machine to run the Hub — a Mac mini, an old desktop, a small PC, or a Raspberry Pi. Then for each screen: an Apple TV, a Pi, or anything that runs a browser. No proprietary boxes, no required bundles. We'll recommend gear, but you can use what you already have.

What happens if my internet goes down?

Nothing. Each player has the next 7+ days of scheduled content cached locally. They keep playing on schedule for as long as the power stays on. When the internet comes back, players quietly sync their heartbeats and pull any new content you've added — no intervention needed.

Can one machine really drive multiple screens?

Yes. A single Hub can drive 4+ screens via HDMI splitters or direct outputs — each one shows independent content. You can also pair separate players (Apple TVs, Pis) anywhere on your network. Most venues mix both.

How do I update content remotely?

From any browser on the same network as the Hub. If you want to manage screens from outside the building, enable optional cloud-relay mode — your media still stays local, you just get a remote control panel.

What about updates and security?

Anchor pushes updates through a stable/beta channel you control. Players self-update silently overnight if you allow it. All sync traffic runs over your local network — no inbound ports, no public exposure required.

How does this compare to ScreenCloud or Yodeck?

They store your media in their cloud and stream playback. If their cloud or your internet goes down, your screens are dead. Anchor stores everything locally on hardware you own. We're built for the moments when the network isn't dependable — which, for most venues, is more often than they'd like to admit.
