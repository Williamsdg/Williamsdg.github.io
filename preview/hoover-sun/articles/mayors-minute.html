The Mayor’s Minute

A summer of progress — and a few good neighbors along the way

A note to Hoover, from the corner office on Municipal Lane.

FB

Mayor Frank V. Brocato

City of Hoover, Alabama

Published May 22, 2026 · A regular column for Hoover Sun readers

This is the season Hoover comes alive. The pools open. The Met fills up on weekends. And our parks are full of kids on bikes who couldn’t care less what day of the week it is — a feeling I think most of us would happily trade a meeting or two to recapture.

It is also when our city does some of its most important work. Paving projects on the south end. New sidewalks running through Trace Crossings. The start of the long-planned safety corridor along Patton Chapel Road, which our team has been mapping, surveying, and listening to neighbors about for nearly two years now.

None of it happens without you.

Progress, the Hoover way

When I took office, I made one promise to myself that I have tried to keep in front of me every day since: we will not be a city that confuses motion with progress. There are plenty of municipalities that announce a great deal and finish very little. Hoover, at its best, is the opposite. Quiet groundwork. Patient planning. And then, suddenly, a finished thing.

From This Week

The Patton Chapel safety corridor will begin construction August 4, adding 1.8 miles of new sidewalk, a protected crossing at Stadium Trace, and four illuminated pedestrian intersections. The project is funded entirely through the 2024 capital plan — no new tax impact — and is expected to be complete by spring 2027.

That is how the Patton Chapel project came together. It is how the new ladder truck for our north-side fire station came together. It is how, by this fall, you will be able to walk safely from Trace Crossings to the Galleria area for the first time in the city’s history.

We will not be a city that confuses motion with progress.

A few good neighbors

One of the privileges of this job is meeting the people who hold a community together — usually quietly, almost never asking for credit. This month alone:

Mary Beth Sanford was named Alabama Teacher of the Year. She has taught fifth grade at Greystone Elementary for fourteen years. The week she found out, she told her class first, before her own family. That tells you everything.

Dr. James Caldwell, who served as principal of Spain Park for two decades, turned 90 this month. Three generations of his former students showed up to celebrate. I have been to a lot of birthday parties in my time. I have never seen anything like that one.

And the Hoover Library Foundation crossed the $4 million mark on a campaign that nobody — including the foundation board — was sure would actually get there. It did, because four hundred and thirty-two of you decided it should.

Thank you

The volunteers. The small business owners. The teachers wrapping up another remarkable school year. The neighbors who showed up to a planning meeting because they had something to say about a sidewalk on a road they have driven for thirty years.

You make Hoover the kind of place people want to come home to. You are why my family chose to raise our kids here. You are why I still walk into City Hall every morning grateful for the work.

Have a good summer. Be patient with the construction. Wave at your neighbors. And when in doubt — buy the kid the snow cone.

— Frank
