Two legislators representing parts of Hoover ousted in Republican primary

A pair of suburban Birmingham incumbents lost their seats Tuesday in a primary that turned on property taxes, school funding, and the future of state-level home-rule legislation.

JA
By Jon Anderson City & State Reporter · Updated May 20, 2026, 11:42 PM
Share ·

Two veteran Republican lawmakers whose districts include portions of Hoover lost their seats Tuesday night in primary challenges that drew unusually heavy turnout for a non-presidential year. Together, the two races represent the largest single-night turnover for the Jefferson County delegation in more than a decade.

State Rep. Marcus Hailey, who had represented District 47 since 2014, was defeated by political newcomer Ashley Crenshaw, a Hoover attorney and former school board member. State Sen. Ron Whittle, a three-term incumbent, fell to challenger Beau Lambert, a small-business owner from Helena. Both winners ran on platforms emphasizing property tax relief and increased local control over school funding decisions.

What happened

Crenshaw outpaced Hailey by a comfortable margin in early returns, with the race called by the Secretary of State’s office shortly after 9:15 PM. Lambert’s margin was narrower, with the AP not making the call until just before midnight.

Turnout across the affected precincts ran roughly 38% higher than the 2022 primary, according to figures from the Jefferson County Board of Registrars — a notable spike for an off-year contest with no statewide marquee race on the ballot.

The issues that moved voters

Property taxes dominated both contests. Hoover residents have voiced growing concern about valuation increases over the last three reassessment cycles, with average homeowner assessments up nearly 22% since 2022. Crenshaw built her campaign around a proposed cap on year-over-year residential reassessment, while Lambert ran on broader cuts to state-level fees and license costs.

Voters told us the same thing in every neighborhood we walked. They love living here, but they’re worried about being priced out by their own success. — Ashley Crenshaw, victory speech

School funding was a secondary but consistent theme. Crenshaw, who served on the Hoover City Board of Education from 2019 to 2023, has been outspoken about what she calls “structural underfunding” from the state for high-growth suburban systems.

What happens next

Both winners will face Democratic challengers in November, but in districts that have run heavily Republican for at least the last four election cycles, both are expected to win comfortably. The Alabama Legislature reconvenes in January, with Crenshaw and Lambert sworn in as part of the 2027 session.

Mayor Frank Brocato issued a brief statement Wednesday morning thanking Hailey and Whittle for their service and pledging to work with the incoming legislators “on the issues that matter most to Hoover families.”

Key Takeaways

  • Both Hoover-area incumbents (Hailey, Whittle) lost their Republican primary races.
  • Turnout was approximately 38% higher than the 2022 primary cycle.
  • Property tax relief and school funding dominated both campaigns.
  • Winners face Democratic opponents in November, but the districts lean strongly Republican.
  • The new legislature is sworn in January 2027.
