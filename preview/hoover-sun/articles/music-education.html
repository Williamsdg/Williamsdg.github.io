Hoover earns national music honor — for the eighth year running

The NAMM Foundation has named Hoover one of the Best Communities for Music Education in Alabama for 2026 — one of only nine systems in the state to make the list.

By Staff Reports May 22, 2026 6 min read Schools · Arts · Community

For the eighth consecutive year, Hoover City Schools has been recognized by the NAMM Foundation as one of the Best Communities for Music Education in the country — an honor that places the district among the top 1% of school systems nationwide for arts access, faculty support, and program funding.

The designation is awarded annually to school districts that demonstrate an outstanding commitment to music education through curriculum, instruction, facilities, and community engagement. Of the more than 130 Alabama school systems, only nine earned the recognition this year.

“Music education is not an extra here. It’s woven into who we are,” said Dr. Amber Cox, director of fine arts for Hoover City Schools. “Every single one of our students has access to a music program from elementary through high school.”

What the recognition measures

NAMM evaluates districts across more than two dozen criteria, including student access to instruments, performance opportunities, the percentage of full-time music educators, and how arts funding has trended over the last three years. Hoover scored particularly high on student-to-teacher ratios and the breadth of its program offerings.

A program built across grade levels

The district’s music programming begins as early as kindergarten and runs through senior year. Elementary students participate in general music classes throughout the week, with opportunities to join honor choirs in upper grades. By middle school, students can choose from band, choir, orchestra, and guitar — with each pathway feeding into Hoover High and Spain Park’s award-winning ensembles.

This year, the Spirit of Hoover Marching Band was invited to perform at the BCS National Championship pregame ceremony in Atlanta. Spain Park’s wind ensemble received a superior rating at state, and the combined Hoover choirs traveled to New York to perform at Carnegie Hall in February.

What it means for families

For parents weighing where to put down roots, music education has become a meaningful differentiator. Hoover’s recognition lands at a time when many districts across the country have scaled back arts offerings under budget pressure — making the district’s eighth-year streak a notable signal of long-term commitment.

“We chose Hoover specifically because of the music program,” said Sarah Latimer, whose daughter plays clarinet at Bumpus Middle. “Knowing that the district treats this as core, not optional, made our decision easy.”

The full NAMM Best Communities list was announced this morning. Hoover joins Alabama systems including Mountain Brook, Vestavia Hills, and Madison in earning 2026 recognition.
