Job-seeking tips

  • Apply in the first few days of a posting. Many employers review applications as they arrive.
  • Use the same words the posting uses for skills and duties, so a quick scan of your résumé finds them.
  • Keep a running file of wins: numbers, thank-yous and problems you fixed. It makes interview answers easy.
  • Keep your availability current on your profile. Employers filter by who can start soon.

Interview prep

0 of 5 read
Answer "tell me about yourself" in 60 seconds4 min

Use three beats: present, past, future. It keeps you from wandering and tells the interviewer where you're headed.

  • Present: your current role in one sentence. "I'm a medical assistant at a four-provider family practice in Homewood."
  • Past: one or two experiences that prepared you for this job. "Before that I ran the front desk at a dental office, so I'm comfortable on both sides of the counter."
  • Future: why this role is the next step. "I'm looking for a clinic where I can take on more patient education."

Say it out loud twice before the interview. Sixty seconds is about 140 words.

The STAR answer for "tell me about a time when"5 min

Behavioral questions want a story with a result. STAR keeps it short.

  • Situation: one sentence of context.
  • Task: what you were responsible for.
  • Action: what you actually did. Use "I", not "we".
  • Result: something concrete. A number, a change, a thank-you.

Prepare three stories before the interview: a problem you solved, a time you handled a difficult person, and a time you learned something fast. Most behavioral questions fit one of the three.

Three questions to ask at the end3 min

"Do you have any questions for us?" is part of the interview. Have three ready.

  • "What does a good first 90 days look like in this role?"
  • "How is the team set up, and who would I work with most?"
  • "What's the next step in your process, and when should I expect to hear back?"

Leave pay and time off for the offer stage unless the interviewer brings them up first.

What to bring and what to wear3 min
  • Two printed copies of your résumé and your list of references.
  • Photo ID and any certificates the posting asked for.
  • A notepad and pen. Writing one thing down shows you're listening.

Dress one step above what people wear on the job every day. For a clinic, that's clean business casual, not scrubs. Arrive ten minutes early, not thirty.

Following up without being a pest2 min

Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours. Name one thing you talked about so it reads as personal.

If the date they gave you passes, send one polite check-in. After that, let it rest and keep applying elsewhere. WorkLift will tell you when the employer views your profile again.
