    The line · since 1847

    A station with a long memory.

    The depot's tracks have seen war, abandonment and the joyous reunion of families. More than 150 years on, the building is serving Auburn again.

    1. 1847
      First depot

      The first depot is built in the heart of what is now downtown Auburn.

    2. 1864
      Rousseau's Raid

      Union cavalry under Maj. Gen. Lovell Harrison Rousseau tear up the depot's tracks, cutting supply lines through Montgomery and Lee County.

    3. 1870
      Rebuilt

      A second depot rises as the golden era of the railroad begins.

    4. 1896
      Wreck Tech

      Students grease the rails before Georgia Tech arrives. The train skids nearly a mile past the station, and the Wreck Tech Pajama Parade is born.

    5. Today
      Now serving

      Restored down to the black-and-white floor tile. The 200-year-old heartwood pine platforms are now the Chef's Table, bar and host stand.

    Sustainability

    Responsibly caught, honestly sourced.

    The Depot was the first restaurant in the state of Alabama to fully qualify for the James Beard Foundation's Smart Catch program, a commitment to serve sustainable and responsibly harvested seafood.

    The kitchen celebrates local and seasonal produce, ethical meat and dairy, and works with vendors including:

    • Stone Hollow Farms
    • Strauss Meats
    • McEwen Farms
    • McEwen & Sons
