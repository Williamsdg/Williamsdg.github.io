Walk-Off Wonders: The Spring's Most Clutch At-Bats

LS Legacy Sports Staff • Updated May 18, 2026 • 5 min read

There is nothing in high school sports quite like a walk-off. The dogpile at home plate, the dugout emptying before the ball even lands, the parents in the stands who'd been holding their breath for three hours — it's the purest jolt of joy the game offers. And this spring's Alabama baseball playoffs gave us a postseason full of them.

We went back through the bracket and pulled the five most dramatic walk-off at-bats of the run — the swings that ended seasons, sent teams to the next round, and will be retold at these schools for years. Counting them down.

5. The Vestavia Squeeze

Sometimes clutch doesn't look like a home run. With the winning run on third and one out in the bottom of the seventh, Vestavia Hills called for a suicide squeeze that nobody in the building saw coming. Sophomore Eli Pruett laid down a perfect bunt up the first-base line, the runner slid in ahead of the throw, and a 2–1 nail-biter was over. Pure nerve.

4. Gardendale Ties It, Then Wins It

Down two in the last inning of a Round 2 elimination game, Gardendale strung together three straight two-out hits before senior Cole Banner roped a double into the gap to clear the bases. The Rockets didn't just win — they erased a deficit with two outs and nobody on, which is the kind of inning that defines a team's belief in itself.

"Two outs, two strikes, season on the line — and the kid fouls off four pitches before he gets the one he wants. That's not luck. That's a hitter."

3. Saraland's Two-Strike Dagger

This one had everything. Tie game, bottom of the eighth, two outs, and Saraland junior Dré Calloway down to his last strike. He fouled off four straight before driving an outside fastball the other way for a clean single, scoring the winning run from second and setting off a celebration that spilled halfway to the outfield. It was the at-bat of the round — a kid refusing to make the last out.

2. Auburn Walks It Off In Extras

A semifinal that went eleven innings deserved an ending to match. Auburn's Jaylen Carter — yes, the same name burning up the football recruiting boards — turned on a 1–1 fastball and sent it over the left-field fence to end a 4–3 marathon and punch a ticket to the championship series. Two-sport stars don't come along often. Ones who deliver in moments like that are rarer still.

1. Mountain Brook's Grand Slam Under The Lights

You couldn't script it better. Bottom of the seventh, bases loaded, two outs, trailing by three in a winner-take-all elimination game. Mountain Brook senior Will Hartsell — a kid who'd gone 0-for-3 on the night — got a hanging breaking ball and didn't miss it. Grand slam. Walk-off. Season saved by a single swing. The video made the rounds across the state within the hour, and it earns the No. 1 spot without much debate.
