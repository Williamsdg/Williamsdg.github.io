Championship History: Revisiting the 2015 Title Run

LS Legacy Sports Staff • Updated Oct 27, 2026 • 7 min read

Walk into the field house and the banner is the first thing you see. 2015 — State Champions. A decade later, it still hangs as the line everyone in the program measures themselves against. Ask any senior on this year's team what they're chasing, and they won't name an opponent. They'll point at that wall.

This is the part of high school sports that doesn't fade with the final whistle. Eleven years on, the 2015 title run isn't a memory — it's the foundation an entire program still stands on. So with another playoff push underway, we went back to revisit the season that started it all, and to find out where those players landed.

The Season That Changed Everything

It didn't look like a championship team in September. The 2015 squad opened 2–2, lost its starting quarterback to an ankle injury in week three, and limped into region play written off by just about everyone — including, a few of them now admit, themselves. Then a sophomore named Marcus Whitfield was handed the keys, and everything changed.

What followed was a nine-game winning streak nobody saw coming. A defense that gave up 30-plus in those early losses suddenly couldn't be moved. The Round 2 game went to overtime. The semifinal was decided by a goal-line stand on the final play. And the title game — a 21–17 classic in front of a packed house — ended with a fumble recovery in the closing minute that's still replayed every December in this town.

"We didn't win because we were the most talented team in the bracket. We won because nobody believed in us but us. That's the part these kids need to understand."

Where They Are Now

The beauty of looking back a decade later is seeing how the story kept going. Whitfield, that sophomore quarterback, went on to play in college and now runs a youth camp every summer — his nephew, by the way, is a four-year starter on this year's varsity, which is a full-circle moment nobody scripted. The star linebacker from that team is a high school coach himself now, two counties over. The kicker who buried the game-winner is a firefighter in town. Most of them still come back for the season opener.

Why The History Matters

It would be easy to treat a championship from over a decade ago as a dusty trophy. But that's not how it works here, and it's not how it should work anywhere. The 2015 team taught the program that talent isn't the whole story — that belief, toughness, and a refusal to fold can carry a flawed roster all the way. Every team since has inherited that lesson, whether they realize it or not.

That's the idea this whole brand was built on: passing the torch. The players who hung that banner did more than win a title — they set a standard, then handed it down. The kids running through the tunnel on Friday nights are carrying something the 2015 team started. And someday, they'll hand it to the next group too.

We sit down with two members of the 2015 championship team to relive the run, swap stories, and talk about what this current squad reminds them of on this week's episode of the Legacy Sports Media podcast. Some history is worth telling again — and again.
