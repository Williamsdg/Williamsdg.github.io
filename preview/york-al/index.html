    Water · Utility System

    Boil water advisory and continued water system maintenance

    Issued December 17, 2025

    Concept example. This is the City's December 17, 2025 notice, shown as text instead of a picture. For today's water status call City Hall at (205) 392-5231.

    Due to low water pressure in the distribution system and a water system outage caused by a pump failure, the City of York Utility System has issued a Boil Water Notice for all water customers.

    What you must do

    Until further notice, boil all tap water used for:

    • Drinking
    • Cooking
    • Making ice
    • Brushing teeth
    • Preparing food

    Bring water to a rolling boil for at least one minute and let it cool before use. You may use bottled water instead.

    Read the full notice

    What this means

    Loss of pressure or system outages can allow contaminants to enter the drinking water system, triggering the need for a boil advisory until testing confirms the water is safe.

    This notice remains in effect until the system has been fully repaired and the water supply has passed bacteriological testing. You will be notified when the advisory is lifted.

    Please help conserve water

    • Take shorter showers
    • Limit outdoor watering and nonessential water use
    • Run only small loads in washing machines and dishwashers
    • Avoid unnecessary water use

        HUD Lead Hazard Reduction & Healthy Homes

        Does your home qualify for lead-safe repairs?

        The City of York, with Service2Sumter, the Alabama Department of Public Health and UA SafeState, was awarded a HUD grant to find and remove lead hazards in older homes across Sumter County.

        Three quick questions

        Is the home in Sumter County?
        Was it built before 1978?
        Does a child under 6 or a pregnant woman live there or visit often?

        What the program can pay for

        • Lead-based paint inspections and risk assessments
        • Paint stabilization, and window and door replacement
        • Temporary relocation during repairs, if needed
        • Healthy Homes repairs for mold, ventilation, pest entry and safety hazards

        How to apply

        1. Ask for the pre-screening form at City Hall, or email Service2Sumter and they will send it.
        2. Bring proof of household income. HUD income limits apply.
        3. Owners and renters can both apply. Landlords agree to the program's terms.

        Questions: info@service2sumter.org, or stop by City Hall.

        About York

        From Old Anvil to York Station to York

        York began at a blacksmith shop called Old Anvil and a stagecoach stop called New York Station. The railroads made it a town, and the name was shortened to York when the City was incorporated on April 6, 1881, the date on the City seal.

        Today York is home to the Coleman Center for the Arts on Avenue A, which fills a former auto shop, mercantile, hardware store and the original Bank of York. The City runs Lake Louise, with boating, fishing and a nature trail, and the City of York Sports Complex.

        Things to see: the Coleman Center, the Open House (a pink house that unfolds into a 100-seat outdoor theatre), the York Historic Caboose, Lake Louise, and the Hometown Christmas parade each December.

        • About 1833New York Station is settled on the Livingston to Gaston stagecoach road.
        • 1838Old Anvil and New York Station merge.
        • 1861The town is renamed York Station as the railroads arrive.
        • 1881Incorporated as York on April 6.
        • 1985The Coleman Center for the Arts is founded under York native Dorothy "Tut" Altman Riddick.
