Prepared for the Mayor, Council and Town Clerk

The same town information, rebuilt around what residents ask for.

The town already publishes the right facts: court day, limb pickup, the fire fee, who runs each department. Most of it sits several clicks deep, and a few pages still carry placeholder text. This concept moves every one of those facts to the front and adds the pieces that are missing.

No obligation. This concept was created independently by Williams Digital, an Alabama web studio. Nobody at Town Hall asked for it and there is no commitment of any kind. Everything shown comes from the town's public website.

What matters more than looks

Four things a town website has to get right.

Accessibility

The Department of Justice now requires local government websites to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. For towns under 50,000 people the date is April 26, 2028. The concept is built to that standard from the first line: keyboard access, readable contrast, labelled forms and real headings.

Phone first

Most residents will open this on a phone. Every task, answer and phone number is designed for a thumb, and the layout was tested on iPhone and Android screens down to the smallest size sold.

Fast on a weak signal

Loaded on a phone, the current homepage is about 1.9 MB across 32 requests. The concept is about 0.6 MB across 9. That difference shows on rural cell service.

Staff can edit it

The clerk's office can post a notice, change a fee or move a meeting date in a few seconds, without calling anyone. Notices come down by themselves when they expire.

What this does for Town Hall.

0

Blank pages

Economic development

A front door for visitors and new business.

The town is already investing downtown: a $262,020 sidewalk and lighting grant on Highway 195, a slab for a farmers market, and a heritage park with a mural wall. People pass through on the way to the national forest and Smith Lake. A Visit section gives them a reason to stop on the square, and a business directory gives local shops a listing the town controls. The current Business page is empty, so this is new ground.

What stays exactly as it is

Water payments stay on the Rural Water site and fines stay on the court payment portal. The concept links to both. Nothing about billing changes and there is no data to move.

What the town would own

The content, the photographs and the design. If the town ever changes vendors, all of it goes with the town.
