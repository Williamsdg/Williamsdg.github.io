The Opportunity

Four things holding the current site back

01

The outdoor brand is invisible

Fort Payne sits at the gate of a National Preserve and one of Alabama's best state parks — and the city site reads like any other municipal template. The single biggest identity in DeKalb County goes unused.

02

Visitor info is scattered

Canyon info lives on a federal site, park info on a state site, dining on review apps. Nobody assembles the trip — which is exactly what the city's site should do, capturing visitors before they arrive.

03

Economic development is buried

Outfitters, lodging, and restaurants want to be where the visitors are. Available property, licensing, and incentives should be one click deep — and this is exactly where cities win grants.

04

The visuals feel dated

A city marketing waterfalls and canyon rims with small static pages. Perception matters — to travelers comparing weekends, and to businesses comparing towns.

More Than a Facelift

What a modern municipal site actually delivers

The visible redesign is the easy part. These are the points that matter to stakeholders, auditors, and grant applications:

ADA Accessible

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA — screen-reader friendly, keyboard navigable, proper contrast. Government sites are held to this standard, and non-compliance is a legal risk.

Mobile First

Visitors plan from a phone at a trailhead and residents pay bills from one. Every page is designed for a phone screen first, desktop second.

Fast Loading

Lightweight pages that load in under two seconds — even on spotty mountain cell coverage.

Staff-Editable

City staff post news, alerts, and trail conditions themselves — no developer, no waiting. A closure notice goes up in minutes.

The Concept

What we'd build — see it live

We've already designed a working homepage concept for Fort Payne, built adventure-first with government organized underneath:

  • Full-screen canyon-and-falls hero: "Adventure Starts Here"
  • Six experience cards — hiking, biking, camping, waterfalls, music history, downtown. Experiences, not department links.
  • "Build Your Adventure" — trip length + interests (including family days and scenic drives) → a suggested day
  • Adventure Dashboard — weather, trail conditions, water levels, park alerts, outdoor events. Nobody else in Alabama is doing this.
  • "Life in Fort Payne" community layer — schools, utilities, parks, jobs, housing, new resident guide
  • "Invest in Fort Payne" featured on the homepage — properties, workforce, infrastructure, success stories
  • "Faces of Fort Payne" — video stories (business owner, park ranger, teacher, restaurant owner, outdoor guide) produced with a broadcast background
  • Council meetings, departments, and emergency contacts

The Payoff

Benefits for the City

Beyond the new look, the redesign changes how the city operates day to day:

  • Mobile-friendly for residents — services work from any phone
  • Faster access to city services — pay, report, and apply in fewer clicks
  • Easier navigation for permits and forms — less counter traffic, fewer calls to City Hall
  • ADA-conscious design — accessible to every resident, and defensible
  • Visitor traffic captured — day trips become overnights, overnights become lodging tax
  • An economic development front door for outfitters, lodging, and restaurants
  • Improved community engagement — events, alerts, and council business residents actually see
Helping Fort Payne Grow

An investment that works for the city

A municipal website isn't an expense line — it's infrastructure for the four things every gateway town is trying to do:

Tourism Promotion

The canyon and falls already draw the crowd. A site that assembles the trip keeps them in Fort Payne for dinner, a show, and a night.

Business Attraction

Outdoor economies attract outfitters, breweries, and lodging. Show them the traffic, the property, and a named contact.

Community Engagement

Events, meetings, and city news residents actually find — participation goes up when information is easy.

Resident Services

Bills paid, issues reported, permits started online — around the clock, without a trip to City Hall.
