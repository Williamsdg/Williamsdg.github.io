01

Give us access to Shopify

Two separate things, and both are needed. The first lets Dylan look at your store; the second lets the new website read your products. Neither can change anything.

A · Add Dylan as staff

  1. In your Shopify admin, click Settings at the bottom of the left sidebar.
  2. Click Users, then Add staff.On some plans this reads "Users and permissions".
  3. Enter Dylan Williams and Dylan@williamsdigital.io.
  4. Under permissions, tick Products, Apps and sales channels, and Themes. Leave Orders, Customers and Finances unticked — we do not need them.
  5. Click Send invite. Shopify emails Dylan directly.

B · Create the Storefront API token

This is the key that lets the new site display your catalogue. If you would rather not do this part, tick Dylan's staff invite above and text him — he can do it himself once the invite is accepted.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps and sales channels.
  2. Click Develop apps.First time only: you'll see "Allow custom app development". Click it and confirm. Only the store owner can do this step.
  3. Click Create an app. Name it Williams Digital Storefront and click Create app.
  4. Open the Configuration tab. Next to Storefront API integration, click Configure.
  5. Tick these boxes, then Save:

    All of these begin with unauthenticated_ in the list:

    • read_product_listings
    • read_product_inventory
    • read_product_tags
    • read_product_pickup_locations
    • read_collection_listings
    • read_content
    • read_metaobjects
    • read_selling_plans
    • write_checkouts
    • read_checkouts
    • write_customers
    • read_customers
    • read_customer_tags
  6. Open the API credentials tab and click Install app.
  7. Copy the Storefront API access token — a long string of letters and numbers.
  8. Paste it into the private document in folder 06 below. Please don't email it.
What this token can and cannot do

It can: read your products, prices, photos, tags, collections and stock levels, and create shopping carts — exactly what a customer browsing your shop can already see.

It cannot: view past orders, see customer records, issue refunds, change prices, edit or delete products, or touch your payouts. You can revoke it at any time by deleting the app.

Your product photos come straight from Shopify — you don't need to send any of those. What we need is the bigger, more atmospheric imagery the new homepage is built around.

The one thing worth getting right

Send the original files, straight from the camera or the phone's photo library — not screenshots, not images saved from Instagram, and not anything already cropped square. The site displays them very large, and a re-saved copy shows it.

05

What happens next

Your current site keeps selling the whole way through. Nothing changes for your customers until you've seen the new one and said yes.

  1. We build privately. The new site goes up on our own address first. Your store is untouched.
  2. We connect your Shopify. Your real products, prices and stock appear on it automatically.
  3. You review it. On a laptop and on your phone. Three rounds of changes are included.
  4. You approve it. Nothing goes live before this, and the final payment isn't due until it happens.
  5. We switch the domain. Scheduled for a quiet hour. Every existing link redirects to the matching new page.
