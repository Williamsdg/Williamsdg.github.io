Where the images live

The question you asked — and the honest answer.

Read this before anything else

The images currently in the store are roughly 500 pixels wide. They are web thumbnails, not print masters. I probed the store for larger originals and there are none. If we migrate what exists today, we are permanently rebuilding Steve's storefront on top of low-resolution files — and once Shift4Shop is switched off, those thumbnails may be all that survives. Getting the high-resolution originals from Steve is the single highest-priority task in this project, and it should happen before we cancel anything.

Recommended — Supabase Storage Recommended

Same account already running his kickoff checklist, plus Lead Finder and the intake system. Nothing new to buy or learn.

  • Cost — 1GB free, then roughly $0.021/GB per month. Even 73 pieces at full print resolution lands well under a dollar a month.
  • Public bucket + CDN for the storefront images, so the site loads fast.
  • Separate private bucket for the print masters, which should never be publicly downloadable — these are the files someone could steal and print from.
  • Automatic resizing on the fly, so one master file serves the thumbnail, the product page and the full-screen view.
Why this over the alternatives

Cloudinary and imgix do image handling beautifully but add another bill and another vendor. Stripe can host a product image, but only one small one per product — nowhere near enough for a gallery. Putting them in the GitHub repo would bloat it and offers no protection for the masters. Supabase is already paid for and already in the stack.

Structure

  • skipper-public — web-optimised display images, 2000px max, served through the CDN.
  • skipper-masters — private, full-resolution originals. Access only through signed links, and only for Steve and the print house.
  • Naming — collection/slug.jpg (e.g. college-sports/9780-paul-bryant-drive.jpg) so files stay findable by hand.
What I already have safe

All 72 catalog images are downloaded and backed up locally, along with a full product list (title, category, slug, image path). If Shift4Shop went away tomorrow, we would not lose the catalog structure. We would only be stuck at 500px until Steve sends originals.

What Stripe does not replace

Shift4Shop is a full store. Stripe is a payment processor. The gap between them is the real work.

Shift4Shop does todayAfter the move
Product catalog & variantsRebuilt as Stripe Products + Prices, or held in Supabase and priced dynamically
CartNeeds building — Stripe Checkout handles one-off carts, but the cart state itself is ours
Shipping calculationStripe Checkout shipping rates. Note: a $35 mini-print and a $4,545 framed canvas do not ship alike — this needs real rules, not a flat rate
Sales taxStripe Tax (0.5% per transaction) or manual Alabama rules
Framing optionsThe product pages reference framing choices — these become Checkout line items or add-ons
Order managementStripe Dashboard covers payments, but Steve loses the "orders" view he knows. Worth a simple admin page
Customer accountsGone unless we build them. Most fine-art buyers check out as guests, so this may be fine
InventoryMatters for limited editions and artist proofs — Stripe does not track stock
Limited editions need real inventory

Artist proofs and numbered limited editions are finite by definition. If two collectors buy edition #12 because nothing tracked the count, that is a genuine problem for a fine artist's reputation — not just a technical bug. Whatever we build has to decrement stock reliably.

Two ways to do this

A · Stripe Checkout + Supabase catalog Recommended

Products, variants, prices and stock live in Supabase. The site renders from that. When someone buys, we create a Stripe Checkout session on the fly.

  • Steve edits his own catalog through a simple admin page — no Stripe dashboard required
  • Avoids hand-creating ~475 Stripe price objects
  • Inventory for limited editions is straightforward
  • Stripe still handles all card data, so PCI scope stays with them

B · Stripe Products as the source of truth

Every artwork and every variant becomes a Stripe Product/Price. The site reads from the Stripe API.

  • Simpler in concept — one system
  • But ~475 prices to create and maintain, and Stripe's dashboard is not built for browsing an art catalog
  • No inventory tracking
  • Realistically only sensible if we cut the catalog down to a smaller set of featured works

Sequence

Ordered so nothing goes dark and no sales are lost mid-move.

01

Collect the originals

Get high-resolution files from Steve before touching anything else. Everything downstream is built on these. Shift4Shop keeps running normally.

02

Confirm the real price list

Export every product, format and price from the Shift4Shop admin and have Steve confirm it. The storefront shows prices, but I would not rebuild a business on scraped numbers.

03

Build the image store

Set up the two Supabase buckets, upload masters, generate web versions. This is safe to do while the old store is still live.

04

Build catalog + checkout

Product pages, variant picker, cart, Stripe Checkout, shipping rules, tax, and an order view for Steve. This is the bulk of the work.

05

Test with real money

Live-mode test purchases at several price points, including a high-value framed canvas. Verify tax, shipping, receipts and refunds before anyone else can buy.

06

Switch over, then wait

Point the store links at the new checkout and redirect old product URLs. Keep the Shift4Shop account open for at least one full billing cycle as a fallback and to preserve order history.

07

Cancel Shift4Shop

Only after real orders have run through Stripe cleanly, and only once we have exported all historical order and customer data.

Do not cancel early

The moment Shift4Shop is switched off, the product data and images hosted there stop being reachable. Export everything first — orders, customers, product data, and every image — and confirm the exports are readable before the account lapses.

Commercial Note

This is outside the current agreement

The signed agreement covers a five-page brochure site for $1,000, with e-commerce explicitly listed as out of scope and all purchasing staying in Shift4Shop. Building a full storefront — catalog, cart, checkout, shipping, tax, inventory, order management — is a substantially larger piece of work and should be quoted separately as its own project.

That is not a reason to avoid it. Owning the whole stack is better for Steve and better for the relationship. It just needs its own scope, its own price, and its own timeline rather than being absorbed into the current build.

Suggested split

Finish and launch the brochure site as contracted, still linking to Shift4Shop. Run the store migration as phase two, on its own agreement, once the high-resolution images are in hand. That gets Steve a better website now and avoids rushing a payment system.
