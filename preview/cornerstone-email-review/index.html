Domain & Email Review

Why your email can’t reach Gmail — and how to fix it

This is the written review included in your website agreement. It explains what I found in your domain’s public settings, what is causing the three email problems you described, and what I recommend doing about each one.

Prepared for Kerissa Grigsby Cornerstone Claims Adjusters, LLC Checked 29 September 2026 By Williams Digital
/ 01 — The short version

One missing setting explains it

Your domain is missing the records that prove an email genuinely came from you. Since February 2024 Google has required every sending domain to carry at least one of them. Yours carries none.

I can show you that rather than just assert it. Below is what Google’s own servers wrote down when an email you sent me on 18 September arrived:

spf=none (google.com: kerissa@cornerstoneadj.com does not designate permitted sender hosts)— and no signature on the message at all

In plain English, Google is saying: nothing about this message proved it came from Cornerstone. It let it through anyway, but it made a note.

So why does my mail from you arrive? Because my address is a business account, and business mail systems generally accept unauthenticated mail and treat it as a mark against you rather than refusing it. Personal gmail.com addresses apply the same rule far more strictly and reject it outright. That is exactly the pattern you described — you can email companies all day, but never anyone on Gmail.

That is not a mistake anyone made. It is a setting that simply was never added, and on a lot of older shared-hosting accounts it never was. It is fixable, and the fix costs nothing in itself.

Your other two problems — the 5 MB attachment limit and the careers@ mailbox you can’t get into — are separate, and they both come from the same place: the old mail service your email still runs on.

/ 03 — Your three problems

What is causing each one

  • “We have never been able to send to Gmail accounts. We can receive, but not send.”

    This is the missing SPF and DKIM. Receiving works because incoming mail is checked against the sender’s settings, not yours — so nothing on your side has to be right for mail to arrive. Sending is the opposite: Gmail checks your domain and finds nothing to verify. Personal Gmail accounts refuse it. Business systems mostly let it through while quietly counting it against you, which is why some of your mail seems fine and some vanishes with no explanation. Fixing this fixes Gmail, and almost certainly improves delivery to carriers and corporate mail systems too, which apply the same checks more quietly.

  • “Anything over 5 MB gets kicked back.”

    That is an attachment cap on the mail service itself, not a DNS setting, so adding the records above will not change it. A 5 MB ceiling is characteristic of an older shared-hosting mail tier. For your work this matters more than it sounds — a set of inspection photographs clears 5 MB easily. Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 both allow 25 MB.

  • “We are supposed to have careers@cornerstoneadj.com, but it’s part of the mess and we can’t access it.”

    That mailbox almost certainly exists on the same service. Getting into it is an account-access problem, not a technical one: someone holds the control panel login for accountsupport.com, and whoever set the mail up originally is the person to ask. If that login can’t be recovered, moving the mail (below) gives you the address back cleanly, because you own the domain.

/ 04 — What I recommend

Two ways forward

These are genuine alternatives, not steps. Option A solves all three problems and is what I would do. Option B solves only the Gmail problem, but it is quick and changes nothing else.

Option A — recommended

Move your email to Google Workspace or Microsoft 365

You keep every address exactly as it is — clay@, kerissa@, careers@. Only the service behind them changes. Cost is in the region of $7–$8 per mailbox per month; worth confirming current pricing before you commit.

  • Authentication is set up properly as part of the move, so the Gmail problem ends.
  • Attachment limit goes from 5 MB to 25 MB.
  • careers@ comes back — as a real mailbox or as a free alias forwarding to Kerissa, whichever you prefer.
  • You hold the administrator login yourself, so you are never locked out of your own mail again. In my view this is the most valuable part.
  • Existing mail can be brought across. Nothing is deleted in the process.

Option B — the minimum fix

Stay where you are and add the three records

Free, and reversible. It requires one thing from your current mail provider: the exact SPF value and DKIM key for your account. They will have these; it is a routine request.

  • Fixes sending to Gmail.
  • Does not fix the 5 MB attachment limit.
  • Does not recover careers@.
  • Leaves your mail on ageing shared hosting, which is what produced these problems.

If you want to stop the bleeding this week and decide about moving later, this is a reasonable place to stand.

A word of caution either way. Email records are easy to get wrong in a way that silently stops all your mail rather than fixing some of it. Whichever route you pick, let me look at the exact values before they go in. Under our agreement this review is advisory — I am not making the changes for you — but I will happily walk whoever does it through it, at no charge.
/ 05 — Separate from email

The domain itself, and the website launch

Your DNS is managed at Wix today, and your current website is hosted there. When the new site goes live, one DNS setting has to change to point the domain at the new site. Your email is untouched by that, as long as the mail records are left exactly alone — which is the single most common way a launch accidentally takes down a company’s email. It will not happen here.

To do that part properly, and to finish the domain half of this review, I still need two things I have not been able to see from outside:

  • Who cornerstoneadj.com is registered with — the company you renew it through. Wix runs your DNS, which does not necessarily mean Wix sells you the domain.
  • Access to that account, or someone in it who can make one change while I watch.

Once I am in, I will check three things that are worth knowing and take two minutes: that the domain is set to auto-renew, that it has a transfer lock on, and that the registration contact is an address you can still get into. A lapsed renewal takes down the website and the email at the same time, and it is the one failure here that would be genuinely expensive.

/ 06 — In one line

If you only take one thing from this

Move the email. The Gmail problem is the symptom you noticed; being locked out of your own careers@ mailbox is the one that should worry you more. Both come from the same old service, and moving off it settles all three at once for about the price of a tank of fuel per month.

Happy to get on the phone with you and your mother and go through any of this — there is nothing here you need to understand technically in order to decide.
