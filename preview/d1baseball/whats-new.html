The redesign around them

Lighter pages

Photos are sized for the screen, ad slots hold their space before the ad arrives, and nothing loads that the reader did not ask for. Ad partners stay; they stop pushing the story around.

See the homepage →
On the site now: On Oct. 3 the homepage made 7,211 network requests to 440 different domains in its first 18 seconds on a desktop browser, logged out.

The story starts on the first screen

On a phone the headline, the byline and the photo are what you see when the page opens. The in-story ad sits between paragraphs at a fixed height.

Open the story page →
On the site now: On a phone the first screen is the header, the menu row, three search boxes and an ad, and the sticky ad at the bottom overlaps the headline.

All 606 games from your Fall Exhibition Central list, grouped by day, on a map, filterable by team and state, with an add-to-calendar button on every game.

Name, role, what they cover, a follow button, and every story with its date and section.

One search for everything

Press the search button (or the / key) and type a team, a writer or a headline. Results appear as you type.

Try search →

Five small things I noticed

  • The rankings page still shows the May 25 poll. Oklahoma, the national champion, is not on it; the final rankings live in a story.
  • “Related News” under a story prints the current story’s author on every card. Mark Etheridge’s Fall Dispatches appears under your name on the Skip Bertman story.
  • The contact page sends Subscription Support to your own address. A self-serve account page (change plan, update card, pause) would take most of that mail away.
  • The 2026 Transfer Portal Leaderboard lives at a web address that says 2025.
  • The footer prints a raw server timestamp under the copyright line.

What stays exactly as it is

  • WordPress stays. This is a new theme, not a new platform.
  • WooCommerce Subscriptions and Memberships stay, with every subscriber, login and renewal date untouched.
  • Authorize.Net keeps processing payments.
  • Mailchimp keeps sending the newsletters.
  • Your ad partners and programmatic revenue stay in place.
  • Every story URL stays the same, so links and search rankings carry over.
  • Stats, Synergy and scores keep coming from the same data.
  • Writers keep publishing the way they do now.

How the numbers were measured: a desktop Chrome window at 1280 pixels wide, logged out, on the evening of Oct. 3, 2026, counting every network response for 18 seconds after the page opened. A story page made 1,327 requests to 280 domains in 8 seconds.
