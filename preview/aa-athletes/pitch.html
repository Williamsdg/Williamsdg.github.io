02 — Why it earns its cost

It is a recruiting tool
before it is a website

An athlete deciding between agencies is comparing promises. Everyone in the room says they will build the athlete's brand. A&A can put a laptop on the table and show them their own site, their own media kit and their own inquiry inbox — already built, already theirs.

That reframes what this costs. It is not overhead spent on athletes A&A already represents. It is the thing that decides which athletes A&A represents next, and it works hardest on the ones who have not signed yet.

In the signing meeting

A demonstration site with the athlete's own name on it, built in advance of the conversation. Very few agencies of any size do this.

In the first ninety days

The site goes live, the media kit starts collecting brand interest, and the athlete sees A&A doing something visible for them immediately.

In year two

A roster of live athlete brands is itself the pitch to the next recruit — and to the brands deciding which agency to call.

03 — Three ways to work together

Pick the one that matches the ambition

Each of these is named for what it achieves, not for how many pages it contains. A&A does not have to start at the top — but the system is designed so that starting small does not mean rebuilding later.

Start here

Single Athlete Launch

One athlete, one domain, one brand — proof that this works before A&A commits the roster.

3–4 weeks
  • Custom athlete brand direction, not a template swap
  • Site on the athlete's own domain
  • Digital media kit with inquiry routing to A&A
  • Stats, film, partners, appearances, optional store
  • A&A holds the domain and the content

For an agency testing the idea on its most marketable athlete.

Biggest impact

Roster Rollout

A group of athletes launched together on a shared foundation, with the A&A dashboard sitting over all of them.

6–10 weeks for the first wave
  • Everything in Single Athlete Launch, per athlete
  • Distinct brand direction for each athlete
  • The A&A Athlete Network hub
  • The A&A dashboard — roster, inquiries, appearances, analytics
  • Per-athlete cost drops materially as the group grows
  • New athletes added in days, not weeks

For an agency that wants athlete technology to be part of the pitch to recruits.

Long game

A&A Digital Platform

A&A sells athlete technology under its own name. Williams Digital builds and runs it in the background.

Ongoing partnership
  • Everything in Roster Rollout
  • White-labelled — the product is A&A's, not ours
  • Brand portal: brands search the roster and submit opportunities
  • Athlete portal: athletes update content and see their own analytics
  • Wholesale build rate per athlete; A&A sets its own client pricing
  • Monthly platform fee covers hosting, support and development

For an agency that wants a proprietary asset, not a vendor relationship.

On numbers. Individual athlete builds land between $700 and $1,500 depending on functionality, commerce and integrations — the same range already sent over. Roster programs are priced per athlete and come down materially with volume. The platform partnership is a build plus a monthly platform fee.

Which of those is right depends entirely on how many athletes A&A wants live and how much of this the agency wants to sell as its own. Worth a conversation about impact before a conversation about price.

04 — Where this goes

Five phases

A&A does not have to use every feature on day one. The point of showing all five is that none of them require throwing away the previous one.

Phase 1

Athlete websites

Profiles, media kits, NIL partners, merch, appearances. Each athlete on their own domain with their own brand. This is where value shows up fastest — an athlete can see their site.

Phase 2

Agency dashboard

A&A manages every athlete from one console. Inquiries and appearance requests stop living in someone's inbox and start living on a record with a clock on it.

Phase 3

Brand portal

Brands browse the A&A roster by sport, market and audience, then submit opportunities directly. A&A becomes the easiest agency in the market to buy from.

Phase 4

Athlete portal

Athletes update their own content, see their own inquiries, and watch their own analytics. Fewer requests land on A&A staff, and athletes feel ownership of the thing.

Phase 5

The A&A platform

At this point it is proprietary software owned by an agency, not a set of websites. That is a different kind of asset — and a different conversation about what the relationship looks like.

05 — Practicalities

What's included, what we'd need

Handled by Williams Digital

  • Design, build, hosting and deployment
  • Domain setup on domains A&A owns
  • Mobile-first — most athlete traffic is a phone
  • Accessibility and performance baseline
  • Content loaded from what A&A supplies
  • Carrying across A&A's existing tools — intake and agreement forms, guides, college directory, career simulation, Scouting HQ and the portal
  • Training so A&A staff can run the dashboard without us

Needed from A&A

  • Athlete list, sports and representation status
  • Final copy for every public page — any placeholder text in the current site gets replaced, not carried across
  • Written athlete consent for name, image and stats
  • Photography, or approval to commission it
  • Verified stats and awards — nothing gets estimated
  • Guardian sign-off for any athlete under 18
  • Confirmation of any certification or membership claim before it appears on a page
  • One person at A&A who owns approvals

A note on accuracy: nothing about a real athlete, and no claim about A&A, goes on a page until A&A confirms it. Every athlete in this concept is invented for exactly that reason. The only A&A facts reproduced anywhere in these screens are the licence number, the Alabama UAAA references and the leagues, all taken from A&A's own published pages.
