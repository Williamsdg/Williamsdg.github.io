02 — Why it earns its cost

It is a recruiting tool
before it is a website

An athlete deciding between agencies is comparing promises. Everyone in the room says they will build the athlete's brand. A&A can put a laptop on the table and show them their own site, their own media kit and their own inquiry inbox — already built, already theirs.

That reframes what this costs. It is not overhead spent on athletes A&A already represents. It is the thing that decides which athletes A&A represents next, and it works hardest on the ones who have not signed yet.

In the signing meeting

A demonstration site with the athlete's own name on it, built in advance of the conversation. Very few agencies of any size do this.

In the first ninety days

The site goes live, the media kit starts collecting brand interest, and the athlete sees A&A doing something visible for them immediately.

In year two

A roster of live athlete brands is itself the pitch to the next recruit — and to the brands deciding which agency to call.

03 — The commercial framework

White-label partnership

As discussed by email on 2 September: A&A owns and manages the athlete relationships, controls positioning and leads account administration. Williams Digital is the development and technical fulfilment partner — implementation, hosting coordination, maintenance, security, integrations and agreed service levels.

Structure

Master agreement first

Executed in advance, with no athlete automatically enrolled or financially committed. Each engagement needs A&A's written designation and, where applicable, the athlete's authorisation, followed by an athlete-specific statement of work.

Revenue

60 / 40

Net platform revenue split 60% Williams Digital / 40% A&A, after mutually approved third-party pass-through costs. Applies to launch fees, recurring subscriptions and standard upgrades. Custom development outside platform scope is quoted separately.

Pilot

Five athletes, then review

A formal operational and commercial review after the first five participating athletes, before broader deployment. Monthly reconciliation; payment within 15 days of month-end.

Working model. A packaged athlete platform at $2,500 launch + $250 per month allocates $1,500 / $1,000 on the launch fee and $150 / $100 monthly — $5,500 in first-year revenue per participating athlete, split $3,300 to Williams Digital and $2,200 to A&A.

Transaction-based revenue — merchandise, paid appearances, sponsorship activations — sits in a separate schedule, since athlete entitlements, agency commissions and third-party rights differ by transaction.

06 — Practicalities

What's included, what we'd need

Handled by Williams Digital

  • Design, build, hosting and deployment
  • Domain setup on domains A&A owns
  • Mobile-first — most athlete traffic is a phone
  • Accessibility and performance baseline
  • Content loaded from what A&A supplies
  • Carrying across A&A's existing tools — intake and agreement forms, guides, college directory, career simulation, Scouting HQ and the portal
  • Training so A&A staff can run the dashboard without us

Needed from A&A

  • Athlete list, sports and representation status
  • Final copy for every public page — any placeholder text in the current site gets replaced, not carried across
  • Written athlete consent for name, image and stats
  • Photography, or approval to commission it
  • Verified stats and awards — nothing gets estimated
  • Guardian sign-off for any athlete under 18
  • Confirmation of any certification or membership claim before it appears on a page
  • One person at A&A who owns approvals

A note on accuracy: nothing about a real athlete, and no claim about A&A, goes on a page until A&A confirms it. Every athlete in this concept is invented for exactly that reason. The only A&A facts reproduced anywhere in these screens are the licence number, the Alabama UAAA references and the leagues, all taken from A&A's own published pages.
