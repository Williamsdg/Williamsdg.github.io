What matters most

Four things a city website has to get right

These matter more than how a site looks.

Accessibility

The Department of Justice now requires city websites to meet WCAG 2.1 AA. For cities Linden's size the date is April 26, 2028. Building to that standard now means it's done well ahead of time.

Phones first

Most residents will open the site on a phone. Every screen of the concept was designed at phone width first.

Speed

A light page loads on a slow rural connection. The concept is a small fraction of the current home page's size.

Staff can update it

Notices, meeting dates and documents are changed by City Hall staff in a simple editor. No web company needed for day-to-day updates.

Benefits for the City

Less time on the phone, fewer trips to the counter

  • Fewer "when is my bill due?" calls. The site shows the dates, including the weekend rule.
  • Fewer surprises at the counter. Residents read that the reconnect fee is paid in person, without a card, before they drive over.
  • One place for notices. A banner the staff can switch on for a boil notice, a closure or a meeting change.
  • Residents know who represents them. Mayor, Council districts and meeting times, always current.
  • Room to grow. Agendas and minutes, forms that submit online, and event pages for ChiLLy Fest can be added when the City is ready.

Helping Linden grow

The first thing a business owner sees

Someone deciding whether to open in Linden, or a family thinking about moving home, will look at the City's website before they call anyone.

Business attraction

U.S. 43, county-seat traffic and a direct line to the Marengo County Economic Development Authority, on the home page.

Visitors and events

ChiLLy Fest, the Parade of Lights, the old courthouse and Chickasaw State Park, with Linden's 200-year story.

Community pride

Ralph Abernathy and Autherine Lucy Foster were both born here. The site says so.

Resident services

The everyday tasks stay first, so the site works for the people who already live here.
