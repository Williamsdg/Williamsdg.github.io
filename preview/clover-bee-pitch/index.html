Why this direction

Your site works. The opportunity is to make it feel like your shop.

There is nothing wrong with the site you have. It sells, it's organised, and your team knows how to run it. What it can't quite do is the thing that actually makes Clover + Bee different — the part where someone walks in, tells you where they're going, and walks out with an outfit they'd never have picked alone.

That's a styling business. Online, it currently looks like a catalogue.

The interesting part is that you're already doing the hard work. Every piece in your store is tagged by colour season, by occasion, by game day, by colour family. That's real merchandising intelligence sitting in your Shopify admin.

A custom front end can put it to work — so the site sorts itself around the customer instead of asking her to know which category she needs.

What changes

Eight things the customer will notice.

  • 01

    A design that's only yours

    Desktop and mobile designed separately, around your photography — not a theme thousands of other shops also run.

  • 02

    Shop by Occasion

    Wedding guest, date night, game day, everyday. She tells the site where she's going, not which category to browse.

  • 03

    Shop My Season, properly

    Your colour-season work becomes an experience: pick a palette and the whole page re-sorts around it.

  • 04

    Game Day as a campaign

    Auburn and Alabama get their own moment instead of sharing one collection page.

  • 05

    Complete the Look

    Curated outfits — dress, necklace, bag, shoes — with one tap to add the whole thing. This is the one that moves order size.

  • 06

    Quick Shop, upgraded

    You have quick-add today. This replaces it with a full sheet: large imagery, sizes and colours, without leaving the page she's on.

  • 07

    Editorial collections

    Lifestyle photography breaks up the grid every eight to twelve products, so a collection reads as curated instead of endless.

  • 08

    Search, cart and motion

    Full-screen search with product images, a slide-out bag with pairing suggestions, and animation used sparingly — only where it helps.

How launch works

Your current site keeps selling the entire time.

  1. Build privately

    The new site is built on my domain, exactly as the concept is now. Nothing touches your live store.

  2. Connect your Shopify

    Read-only connection to your real products, prices and inventory. Your admin is never modified.

  3. Review and revise

    You and your team walk through it, on a laptop and on a phone. Two rounds of revisions included.

  4. Approval

    Nothing goes live until you've signed off on it. Final payment is due at this point, not before.

  5. Domain switch

    Scheduled at a quiet hour. Every existing product and collection link redirects to the matching new page, so your search rankings carry over.

  6. Little to no downtime

    Checkout continues running on Shopify throughout — a customer mid-purchase during the switch is unaffected.
