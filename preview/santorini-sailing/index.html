A real sailing company

When the wind is there, the sails go up.

There are a lot of boats in Santorini, and most of them motor. We are the island's first sailing company, and we still sail — which is why guests notice the difference the moment the engines go quiet.

The rest of it follows from the same instinct. Every cruise leaves from Vlichada on the south side of the island, not because it is convenient but because it is the safest marina for getting guests on and off a boat. Group sizes stay small on purpose. The food is cooked in front of you, on board, while you sail.

First on the island

Sailing Santorini since 1987, under the name of Capt. Ted — and holding the standards that come with having been first.

Small groups, on purpose

Ten to eighteen guests on most cruises. Enough people to make a good day of it, few enough that the deck never feels crowded.

Licensed and equipped

GNTO-licensed, with life jackets, floating noodles and a life raft carried on every vessel as Greek maritime law requires.

The journey, not the stop

We are driven by the experience on board rather than by ticking off destinations. The swim stops matter; so does the sailing between them.

What the fare covers

You pay once, at the dock.

Transfers, food, drink and equipment are in the price on every cruise. The only thing that varies between boats is the menu and whether we hand you a towel.

Simple, Greek, homemade

The food is half the reason people come back.

Dine on board and it should feel like eating in a Greek home — because that is how it is cooked. You can watch the chef work while you sail.

Butcher-cut meats and vegetables are delivered daily, marinated and grilled to order. Refreshing salads, appetisers, baked vegetable dishes and seafood delicacies come out alongside. Recipes have been sharpened over decades of cooking them in the same galleys.

Getting there

Everything leaves from Vlichada.

All cruises start on the south side of the island, at Vlichada marina — the safest harbour on Santorini for getting guests on and off a boat. Your transfer is included and your pickup point comes with your confirmation.

How booking works

  1. Pick your cruise and dateChoose daytime or sunset, semi-private or private, and check live availability on the booking system.
  2. Confirmation by emailYou get written confirmation. Check the details are right and tell us straight away if anything is wrong — including allergies.
  3. Pickup detailsYour pickup place and time window arrive with the confirmation, pinned on Google Maps. Give us an email and a phone number so we can reach you about delays.
  4. Board at VlichadaMeet the crew, stow your bag, and the boat leaves on the hour.

Concept — not yet built

The part of the day we can't control is the road.

Nearly every hitch on a cruise day happens before anyone reaches the water: a driver running late in high season, a guest waiting on the wrong corner, a guest wondering whether the wind has cancelled anything. A companion app is the obvious next step — and it does not exist yet.

Sketched here so the idea is concrete. Nothing on this screen is live, and none of it is available to book against today.

Pickup, pinned

The exact corner on a map, the time window, and the driver's name to reconfirm against.

Live departure updates

A push when the van leaves, when it's five minutes out, and when boarding opens at Vlichada.

Weather notifications

Early warning when conditions look like changing a route — before guests are already in a taxi.

Itinerary as it happens

The stops for your boat, in order, so guests know whether the next anchor is a swim or lunch.

A line to the crew

Before you book

The questions we get asked most.

Cancellation

Every cancellation request is reviewed by the reservations team, who reply by email within three days.

How long is a cruise, and when does it leave?

Every cruise runs daily, as either a day or a sunset sailing, and lasts roughly four and a half to five hours. Day cruises board at 10:00 and are back alongside at 15:00. Sunset cruises board at 15:00 and finish after the sun goes down.

Can we book a full day or an overnight charter?

Yes, on a private basis and by request. A full day runs from 10:00 until after sunset. An overnight charter starts at 10:00 and finishes at the same time the following day. An overnight sleeps four couples — eight guests — per vessel.

Which swimming stops and sights are included?

Every cruise stops at the volcanic warm springs and anchors at well-known beaches and secluded bays for swimming and snorkelling. Time at each stop depends on conditions and usually runs 20 to 30 minutes. Exact stops vary by boat — open “Route & timings” on any cruise above to see its order.

What should we bring?

Whatever you'd take to the beach:

  • Hat
  • Sunglasses
  • Swimsuit
  • Sun block
  • Camera
  • A light sweater for afternoon cruises
  • A towel, if you're joining the Deluxe or Dreamcatcher
Are towels provided on board?

Towels are provided on all Exclusive and private cruises. Guests joining the Deluxe or Dreamcatcher cruises should bring their own.

Is snorkelling equipment included?

Yes. A mask and snorkel are available for every guest on board, and floating noodles are carried on all our vessels.

Are vegetarian options available?

Every menu is balanced to include vegetarian dishes without you having to tell us in advance. If you have an allergy or a dietary restriction, let us know when you book or as far ahead of the cruise as you can.

Are there life jackets on board?

Under Greek maritime law, every charter vessel carries the required number of life jackets and a life raft. We also reserve the right to change stops for weather, force majeure or safety.

What happens if the transfer is running late?

Santorini's roads are not famous for being quick, and delays do happen in high season. Please be patient and call the office on (+30) 22860 21380 for an update. Reconfirm your name with the driver before you get in — several companies run pickups on the same routes, and guests occasionally leave with the wrong one. After the cruise, check your return transfer with the crew, as the vehicle and driver may have changed.
