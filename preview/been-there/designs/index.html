Design rules

What we can draw — and what we can't.

A place name is free to use. Some landmarks, lighting designs, slogans and transit logos are owned — and a couple of countries charge for commercial images of their monuments. These rules keep every design sellable everywhere.

Go

Use freely in any design.

  • Place names — cities, islands, countries, regions
  • Coordinates, compass roses, maps we draw ourselves
  • Generic scenes: white houses & blue domes, boats, palms, mountains, trams, balloons
  • Traditional patterns: Greek key, azulejo, seigaiha waves, zellige stars, knit yokes
  • Local words: Yamas, Saudade, Arigatō, Pura vida
  • Our own photos (or stock with an extended/merch license)
  • Google Fonts (open license covers merch)

Caution

Stylize heavily or skip. Never a recognizable copy.

  • Italian monuments (Colosseum, David, Duomo) — Italy's heritage code requires permission & fees for commercial images
  • Greek archaeological sites (Acropolis, Parthenon) — commercial use needs a Ministry of Culture license
  • Modern buildings still under copyright — check the architect's death date
  • Creative Commons photos — attribution/share-alike terms make them a bad fit for products
  • Phrases other brands use on clothing — search the USPTO first

Never

Owned. Licensing required or not available.

  • Eiffel Tower lit up at night (the light show is protected)
  • Sydney Opera House shape & image (registered trademark)
  • Hollywood Sign (licensed by the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce)
  • London Underground roundel & transit branding; skip “Mind the Gap”
  • “I ♥ NY”, tourism-board logos & slogans
  • Hotel, bar, resort & shop logos — the Hard Rock-style shirts people actually lost
  • Sports teams, universities, airlines
