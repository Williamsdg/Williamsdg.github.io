German traditions

Two records, one park

Cullman was founded by German immigrant John G. Cullmann in 1873. The market brings two Ore Mountain traditions home, built bigger than anywhere else.

World's largest

The Schwibbogen

A German candle arch, born among Ore Mountain miners longing for daylight. Cullman's is the largest walk-through Schwibbogen in the world, bigger than Dresden's Striezelmarkt arch. Walk under it, then cross the walkway on top for a view of the whole market.

North America's tallest

The Weihnachtspyramide

A rotating wooden Christmas pyramid. Most sit on a table. Cullman's stands over 40 feet tall beside the Cullman County Museum, and it's lit every evening of the season.

Who is the Christkind?

The market's namesake

The Christkind, or Christ Child, is the traditional gift-bringer of German-speaking countries, much as Santa Claus is here. The custom took hold during the Reformation, when Martin Luther moved the focus from St. Nicholas Day to Christmas itself. A Christkindlmarkt is the market held in its honor through Advent.

Eat, drink & shop

Glühwein & chimney cakes

Food and drink huts serving German market classics, plus handcrafted gifts from makers like Christian Ulbricht, Dregeno Seiffen, Richard Glässer and Ino Schaller.

Inside the Festhalle

Käthe Wohlfahrt

Germany's family-owned Christmas retailer since 1964, in a 3,500 sq ft store inside the Festhalle Farmers Market. Featuring Christian Ulbricht nutcrackers, gnomes and wooden figures.
