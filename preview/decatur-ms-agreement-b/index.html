3.The Billing Software Question

Read this before choosing Option BFour portal features — account balance, due date, usage history and the high-usage warning — can only work if the Town's water billing software can produce a regular export of balances and readings. Most can. Some older systems cannot. Williams Digital has not yet been told which software the Town uses, so this cannot be confirmed today. If it turns out the system cannot export, those four features are not built, and the one-time fee for Option B is reduced by $400 and the monthly by $10 — the Town is not charged for something it did not receive. Every other portal feature works regardless. The answer is a single question to whoever supports the billing system, and it should be settled in the first week.

4.Not Included

  • The staff dashboard. The Town runs its administration through BBI and is keeping it. The dashboard shown in the concept is not part of this agreement and is not being charged for.
  • Utility billing software, meter reading, or a replacement for the Town's billing system. The portal reads from the Town's system; it does not become it.
  • Card processing. Payments continue to run through the Town's existing providers, who are paid directly by the Town or the resident. Williams Digital never touches card data and adds no fee.
  • Court case management or a public docket. Court payment is by citation number only.
  • Codification or recodification of the Town's ordinances. That is specialist legal publishing work, typically $8,000–$11,000, and Williams Digital will refer the Town to firms that do it.
  • Remediating the Town's existing PDF documents for accessibility. The new site meets WCAG 2.1 AA; documents the Town uploads are the Town's responsibility. Williams Digital will quote this separately if the Town wants it.
  • A native app in the App Store or Google Play. The website and portal run in the phone's browser.
  • Domain registration fees, the Town's Google Workspace email, paid advertising, or an SEO retainer.
  • Photography, logo design or a new Town seal. Quoted separately if the Town wants it.

5.Accessibility & Public-Sector Obligations

The website and portal will be built to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, the standard the U.S. Department of Justice adopted for Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act. For a public entity serving fewer than 50,000 people, the compliance date is April 26, 2028. Building to the standard now means the Town is not doing it again later under a deadline.

Williams Digital will not install an accessibility overlay widget. Those are the pop-up accessibility toolbars sold as a one-click fix. They do not satisfy Title II, and in 2025 the Federal Trade Commission fined one such vendor $1,000,000 over claims that its widget made sites compliant. Accessibility here is built into the pages themselves.

This website does not satisfy the Town's legal publication requirements. Under Mississippi law, notices that must be published in a newspaper must still be published in a newspaper. The website is in addition to that, never instead of it.

Records retention remains the Town's responsibility under the Mississippi Department of Archives and History schedules. The website publishes documents; it is not the Town's records system and should not be relied on as one.

Williams Digital will provide an accessibility statement for the site and will correct accessibility defects in delivered work at no charge during the support period.

6.The Domain & Who Holds the Keys

This matters more than the website and it is true no matter who the Town hires. The Town's .gov domain and the administrator account for its email are currently held by the Town's existing website vendor, not by the Town. The .gov registration shows a renewal date of November 3, 2026.

Before launch, someone employed by the Town must hold administrator access to the domain. DNS is not equipment and costs nothing — it is a setting inside the domain account, and once the Town has the account the setting is already there. Williams Digital will walk the Town through it on a screen share at no charge, or, if the Town prefers, the Town may add Williams Digital as an additional administrator and Williams Digital will make the change.

The Town holds the top-level access at all times. Williams Digital will never be the sole holder of the Town's domain, email or registry access, and any access the Town grants may be revoked by the Town at any time, including during this engagement.

11.Resident Data

The portal holds names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, service account references and problem reports belonging to residents of the Town.

The Town owns and controls all data collected through the website and portal. Williams Digital provides and maintains the mechanism only, and will not use, sell, share, or train any system on that data, and will not contact residents for any purpose of its own.

Williams Digital will delete any individual record, or all stored records, within ten (10) business days of a written request from the Town, and will provide a complete export of the data at any time on request, in a common format, at no charge.

Access to resident data is limited to Williams Digital personnel who need it to perform this work. Williams Digital will notify the Town in writing within seventy-two (72) hours of becoming aware of any unauthorised access to resident data.

If the Town cancels the build after work has begun, the deposit is non-refundable and all work completed to that point is delivered to the Town.

If Williams Digital cancels, the Town receives all work completed to date, a full data export, and a pro-rata refund of any amount paid for work not performed.
