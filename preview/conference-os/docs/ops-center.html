Module 01 · Flagship

Live Game Ops Center.

The air-traffic-control view of every SAC game in progress. One screen, every game, every status — score, officiating crew, replay activity, weather, incidents, broadcast windows, and crew grading. Designed to be the page that's never closed on Saturday.

01 · WHAT IT DOES

One screen that answers: "what's happening across the conference right now?"

Today, on a Saturday afternoon, the SAC office has to assemble its situational awareness from broadcast feeds, school AD text threads, replay center phone calls, and weather-app notifications. Nobody has a single view of the conference. The Ops Center fixes that.

The screen is anchored by a game board showing every live SAC game with its score, quarter, officiating crew, broadcast partner, and a row of status icons (replay active, weather, incidents, security). Click any game and the right side opens the game detail panel with that game's timeline, on-field crew, replays in progress, and conference-office notes.

Surrounding the game board are five secondary panels: replay activity across the conference, the weather radar for every host venue, the active-incident log, the broadcast window status for every partner, and a live communications feed showing the crew and replay channels. A "Wall Mode" toggle removes all UI chrome so the page can be projected on the 4K monitors in the conference office.

✓

The litmus test for this module.

If you walk into the SAC office on a Saturday at 3 PM and ask the senior person on duty "what's the status of every game right now?", they should be able to answer it in under 10 seconds by glancing at one screen. That's the design target.

02 · WHO USES IT

Owned by the conference office. Visible to anyone with operational reason.

Day-to-day owner

The senior person on duty in the conference office on game day — typically the Coordinator of Football Operations during the football season, and the analogous sport coordinator otherwise. They're the human in the loop watching the wall.

Always-on viewers

  • SAC executives (Commissioner, Deputy Commissioner, VPs) — open as needed on phones/laptops to check status without interrupting the on-duty team.
  • Sport administrators — filtered to just their sport, watching for officiating and incident escalations they need to be informed about.
  • Replay center leads — Portsmouth and Capital City primary, with cross-cover sites — see the games they're staffing alongside the on-field crews.
  • Broadcast partner liaisons — see the broadcast windows and incidents that affect them (a weather hold means a programming pivot).
  • Joint-command security leads — see incidents in real time, can open the Incidents module from a click.

Read-only by default

The Ops Center is mostly read-only — the data it shows comes from other systems. The only direct actions are: open an incident, message a crew, escalate to leadership, and toggle Wall Mode.

03 · DATA SOURCES

The Ops Center is a composite view. Almost no data is born here.

Every panel on the screen is a real-time read of a system of record somewhere else. The Ops Center's job is to compose them into a single picture — not to own the underlying data.

PanelSource of truthRefreshNotes
Game board — score & clock(every game tile) NCAA Genius Sports live data feed (primary) · SAC Sports feed (fallback) REAL-TIME WebSocket push. ~2s latency to UI.
Crew assignments(per game) Officials & Replay module (Module 02) EVENT Pulled when game tile loads, updated if crew changes.
Replay activity(reviews in progress) Replay center system (Portsmouth + Capital City + cross-cover) REAL-TIME Replay timeline events pushed as ruling stages advance.
Weather radar(per venue) NWS API + venue-side lightning detection feeds REAL-TIME Lightning advisories auto-trigger a SEV-2 incident.
Incident log(active) Incidents module (Module 08) REAL-TIME Opening an incident from here writes to the incidents store.
Broadcast window status(per partner) Broadcast partner feeds (Apex, Vista Networks, SACN, SACN+) EVENT "On Air" / "Halftime" / "Up Next" pushed by partner ops.
Crew live grading(per game) Officiating grading engine (sits in Module 02) REAL-TIME Provisional · finalized after game in Officials module.
Field communications feed Comms Hub crew + replay channels (Module 04) REAL-TIME Filtered to operational message types only.
School AD acknowledgments School Portal (Module 12) EVENT Shown when a school sends a request or ack.
Attendance / capacity(per venue) School ticketing systems (Ticketmaster, Paciolan) 15-MIN Pulled from school-side APIs; close enough to live for ops.

Data flow at a glance

FROM OWNERS · TO OPS CENTER · TO LEADERSHIP
Game feeds → Replay center → Weather (NWS) → Composer
Officials module → Incidents module → Broadcast partners → Composer
Composer → Game board → Right-side panels → Wall Mode display
!

The Ops Center is not a system of record.

If a number on the Ops Center is wrong, the fix is upstream — at the source module. The composer reflects, it doesn't store. This matters when leadership wants to "edit a score" or "remove an incident" from the screen — the action belongs at the source.

04 · MONITORING APPROACH

The Saturday rhythm. Who watches what, when.

Pre-kickoff (T-3 hours)

On-duty coordinator opens the Ops Center on the wall display. Confirms every game tile shows the correct crew, replay center, and broadcast partner. Walks through the venue list with the local-PD liaison to verify joint-command standing. The page reflects whatever Scheduling (Module 05) has published; misconfigurations are usually a Scheduling issue, not an Ops Center one.

Game-window operation (T+0 to T+5 hours)

The wall stays on the game board. The on-duty coordinator watches for the four things that demand a human response:

  • Status changes on the game tiles — a game flips from LIVE to WX HOLD or to DELAY. A weather hold auto-creates an incident; the coordinator opens it and confirms joint command is engaged.
  • Replay activity stage transitions — when a replay sits in REVIEWING for > 90s, the new comms protocol applies. The coordinator confirms the replay center has notified the field referee.
  • Sentiment routing notifications from Social Listening — a crew mention spike opens an Incident (SEV-MON). Coordinator reviews and decides whether to escalate.
  • School AD requests appearing in the activity feed — usually a film clip submission or a media availability request. Routed automatically, but the coordinator sees them appear.

Post-game (T+5 to T+24 hours)

Games settle to FINAL. The coordinator confirms every game closes cleanly: replay tallies match, all incidents are either closed or moved to monitoring, and the game's after-action report is queued in Incidents.

✓

The "Wall Mode" pattern.

Wall Mode rotates between the game board (45 seconds) and the conference map (15 seconds) on a 4K display in the conference office. No chrome, no clutter. It's designed so anyone walking by can read it from across the room — and so the on-duty coordinator never has to click a tab to see the other view.

05 · KPIs

What this module makes measurably better.

The Ops Center is a composite view — its KPIs are about visibility, response, and the elimination of "what's going on?" as a question.

Time-to-situational-awareness ≤ 10 seconds Person walks in. They glance at the wall. They know. Today: 3-5 phone calls
Incident detection-to-acknowledgment ≤ 3 minutes From the moment a weather hold or medical event is flagged to a human ack. Target: holding steady at 3.1 min
Replay decision visibility 100% of rulings logged Every confirm/overturn shows up on Ops Center within 30s of ruling. Target: 100% (currently varies)
Cross-module routing accuracy 95% auto-routed Of all flagged events, 95% reach the right SAC team without manual intervention. Today: largely manual

What leadership should ask in monthly review

  1. How many incidents this month? How many SEV-1 / SEV-2 vs. SEV-3 / SEV-4?
  2. What was the longest time-to-acknowledge? What caused the delay?
  3. Did any school complain that we didn't see something fast enough? If yes, root-cause it.
  4. How many times did Wall Mode get used? (Proxy for "is this actually the room's primary view?")
