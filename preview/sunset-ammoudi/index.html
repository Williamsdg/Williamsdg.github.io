Under the red cliffs of Oia, in a cove that seems forgotten in time.

Right at the edge of Oia's old port, where the fishermen's caiques arrive early every morning with the day's catch, is where you'll find the most famous lobster spaghetti of the Cyclades — and the sea, reaching out in every direction.

Paraskevas and Margarita opened the first fish taverna in Ammoudi in the early 1980s. The rough volcanic soils of Santorini bear their own gifts, and together with what the sea brings in, they become dishes that speak to every palate. No pretences, no needless decoration — just the savage beauty of Ammoudi and the tastes of a family kitchen.

The people behind Sunset

Paraskevas& Margarita

Paraskevas Birigkos arrived in Santorini in 1979 and met his wife, Margarita. An avid fisherman with a sea full of fish on his doorstep, his daily catch reached fifty to sixty kilos — far more than one family could eat.

So in the early '80s the two of them opened the first fish taverna in Ammoudi. There was no road down here then. Supplies arrived on the back of a donkey named Andrew. The local fishermen came first; the rest of the world followed.

Margarita still cooks. She adds her touch to every traditional dish that leaves the kitchen — which is why the lobster pasta carries her name on the menu.

You may hear the locals refer to Sunset as “Paraskevas'” instead. That is to show how loved and respected Paraskevas is. From the taverna's own history
The authentic side of Santorini

Greetings from Ammoudi Bay

Dug into the red volcanic rock beneath Oia, Ammoudi was the island's main port through the 19th and 20th centuries — Oia's ships carried Vinsanto as far as Russia. Today you reach it down 214 steps from the castle, or by the road that came much later.

Come for the food.Stay for the sunset.

Ammoudi sits directly beneath the most photographed sunset in Greece — and unlike the crowd on the castle above, you get to watch it sitting down, with a glass of Assyrtiko.

01 — The SeaCaught this morning

The fishermen's caiques tie up a few metres from the tables. What they bring in is what you'll be offered, by the kilo, the same day.

02 — The TableA family kitchen

Santorini fava, tomato fritters, white aubergine from the island's volcanic soil, and the lobster pasta that carries Margarita's name.

03 — The SunsetAt the water's edge

The cliff turns from ochre to red, the catamarans come back across the caldera, and the light goes down into the Aegean in front of you.
