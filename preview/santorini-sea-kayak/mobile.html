The mobile layout

The same page, rendered at phone width. The mobile view is designed rather than reflowed: choosing a tour comes first, price and difficulty stay visible while you scroll the comparison, directions and the phone number are one tap away, and a persistent Book bar sits below the content instead of on top of it.

What changes on mobile

  1. Tour choice comes firstBelow the hero, the filter row scrolls sideways instead of stacking into four rows of chips, so the first tour card is visible almost immediately.
  2. Price and level stay on the cardEach card keeps the starting price in the corner of the photo and the four-bar difficulty meter above the link — the two things people actually compare.
  3. The comparison table scrolls, anchoredThe tour-name column is sticky, so duration, age, group size and the three price columns scroll underneath it without losing which row you are reading.
  4. Directions in two tapsThe menu carries “Directions to the base” and a tap-to-call button; the meeting-point section opens Google Maps directly.
  5. A book bar that doesn't cover anythingThe bar is fixed to the bottom and the page reserves the same height below the footer, so nothing is ever hidden behind it. It also names the tour you last opened.
  6. Booking as a sheetThe booking panel slides up from the bottom on phones and in from the right on desktop — same markup, same estimator, different gesture.
