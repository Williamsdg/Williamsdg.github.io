Chapter One

The Harper Lee Legacy

❧

Nelle Harper Lee grew up on Alabama Avenue, two doors from a boy named Truman who would become Capote. From this square-mile of small-town Alabama came To Kill a Mockingbird — and the town has been keeping the story ever since. Each spring, townspeople stage the courtroom scenes in the very courthouse that inspired them, before audiences from every state and dozens of countries.

  • The Old Courthouse Museum — the model for the most famous courtroom in American fiction
  • The annual Mockingbird play, performed by Monroeville's own
  • Literary landmarks within a short walk of the square

Chapter Two

Visit Monroeville

❧

Plan a day or a weekend around the square: tour the Old Courthouse Museum, trace the Capote and Lee homesites, browse the shops and cafés downtown, and time your trip to the spring play — tickets sell out across the country. Literary pilgrims have been finding their way here for sixty years; the city finally has a front door worthy of the trip.

  • Old Courthouse Museum tours & exhibits
  • Downtown dining, shops, and galleries on the square
  • Festivals, author events, and the spring production

Chapter Three

Start A Business

❧

Tens of thousands of literary travelers pass through Monroe County every year — and every one of them eats, shops, and stays somewhere. The city's business office helps you put your storefront in their path: licensing walked through in plain language, available properties on and around the square, and incentives for the historic district.

  • Business licensing, walked through step by step
  • Available storefronts and commercial property listings
  • Historic district façade & tourism business incentives

Chapter Four

Call It Home

❧

Front porches, oak-lined streets, and a square where people still know your name — the small-town Alabama that Lee wrote about is still here to live in. Historic homes at prices that surprise newcomers, county schools, and a community that shows up for each other, spring play or Friday night.

  • Historic neighborhoods within a walk of the square
  • Schools, churches, parks, and youth leagues
  • New resident guide — utilities, trash day, and who to call
