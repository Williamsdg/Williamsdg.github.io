The Opportunity

Four things holding the current site back

i.

The literary brand is invisible

Harper Lee, Capote, the courthouse, the play — the most marketable civic identity in Alabama appears nowhere compelling on the city's own site. Visitors research the museum; the city is absent from that journey.

ii.

Tourism info is scattered

The museum has one site, events another, dining reviews a third. Nobody assembles the visit — plan a day, park once, walk the landmarks — which is exactly what a destination town's site should do.

iii.

Economic development is buried

Tens of thousands of visitors pass through annually, and a business owner who wants to serve them can't find available storefronts, licensing steps, or a contact. That's lost downtown investment.

iv.

The visuals feel dated

Generic municipal layouts do a disservice to a town this distinctive. Perception matters — to literary travelers comparing trips, and to families and businesses comparing towns.

More Than a Facelift

What a modern municipal site actually delivers

The visible redesign is the easy part. These are the points that matter to stakeholders, auditors, and grant applications:

ADA Accessible

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA — screen-reader friendly, keyboard navigable, proper contrast. Government sites are held to this standard, and non-compliance is a legal risk.

Mobile First

Visitors plan trips from a phone and residents pay bills from one. Every page is designed for a phone screen first, desktop second.

Fast Loading

Lightweight pages that load in under two seconds — including on rural Monroe County connections.

Staff-Editable

City staff post news, alerts, and events themselves — no developer, no waiting. Play-season updates go up in minutes.

The Concept

What we'd build — see it live

We've already designed a working homepage concept for Monroeville — a city site structured like the story it keeps:

  • The Monroeville Storybook — five chapters with a reading-progress ribbon: Discover Monroeville, The Literary Legacy, Visit Downtown, Build Your Future Here, City Services
  • Government becomes part of the story instead of the entire story
  • Literary typography — drop caps, chapter numerals, an illustrated courthouse hero
  • The Literary Trail — an interactive map: courthouse, Lee & Capote landmarks, museums, shops, restaurants
  • Plan Your Visit — pick Literary Tour, Weekend Trip, Family Visit, or Historic Tour and get suggested stops
  • "Stories From Monroeville" — short video portraits (bookseller, teacher, author, family, leader), rotated monthly, produced with a broadcast background
  • "Why Monroeville" — location, downtown, community, workforce, available properties for investors
  • Council agendas, departments, and emergency contacts

The Payoff

Benefits for the City

Beyond the new look, the redesign changes how the city operates day to day:

  • Mobile-friendly for residents — services work from any phone
  • Faster access to city services — pay, report, and apply in fewer clicks
  • Easier navigation for permits and forms — less counter traffic, fewer calls to City Hall
  • ADA-conscious design — accessible to every resident, and defensible
  • Literary tourism finally captured — longer visits, more meals, more nights booked
  • A downtown recruitment tool — storefronts filled around visitor traffic
  • Improved community engagement — events, alerts, and council business residents actually see
Helping Monroeville Grow

An investment that works for the city

A municipal website isn't an expense line — it's infrastructure for the four things every destination town is trying to do:

Tourism Promotion

Literary pilgrims already come. A site that assembles the visit turns day-trippers into overnight guests — and overnight guests into lodging tax.

Business Attraction

Visitor traffic is a market. Show available storefronts, licensing steps, and a named contact, and downtown fills in around it.

Community Engagement

Events, meetings, and city news residents actually find — participation goes up when information is easy.

Resident Services

Bills paid, issues reported, permits started online — around the clock, without a trip to City Hall.
