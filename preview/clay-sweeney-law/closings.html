Residential closings, start to recorded deed.

What happens after the contract is signed, what to bring on the day, and what the state will charge to record it.

Your side of the table

What to bring, and what the office handles.

Bring to a purchase closing

  • A current government-issued photo ID for every person signing.
  • Your funds to close, by wire or certified funds, in the amount on your final figures. Confirm wiring instructions by phone before you send anything.
  • Proof of homeowner's insurance, if your lender has not already received it.
  • Everyone who will be on the loan or the deed. If someone cannot attend, say so early; a power of attorney has to be approved by the lender ahead of time.

What the office does for a buyer

Examines the title so you know what you are buying is free of someone else's debts. Works with your lender on the loan documents and figures. Explains each document before you sign it. Records your deed and mortgage, and sees that your owner's title policy is issued.

Bring to a sale closing

  • A current government-issued photo ID for every owner on the deed.
  • Your mortgage lender's name and loan number, sent ahead of time so a payoff can be ordered.
  • Keys, garage remotes and any gate or alarm codes.
  • Contact details for the homeowners' association, if there is one.
  • Bank details or a forwarding address for your proceeds.

Tell the office early if…

An owner cannot attend, an owner has died, the property is held in a trust or an estate, or there has been a divorce since you bought. None of these stops a sale, but each needs paperwork that takes time. Clay also handles probate, so an inherited property can be dealt with under one roof.

Start a sale closing

Bring to a refinance closing

  • A current government-issued photo ID for every borrower.
  • A recent statement for the mortgage being paid off.
  • Your homeowner's insurance details.
  • Any funds due at closing, confirmed by phone before you wire.

Good to know

When you refinance your primary residence, federal law generally gives you three business days after signing to change your mind, so the loan does not fund on the day you sign. Your lender will confirm whether that applies to you.

Contract to keys

How a closing moves through the office.

Every deal is different, but the order of work is the same. Knowing it makes the quiet weeks less worrying.

Open a file

  1. Step one

    The contract arrives

    From your agent, your lender or you. A file is opened and the title work is ordered.

  2. Step two

    Title is examined

    County records are searched for mortgages, liens, judgments, unpaid taxes, easements and breaks in the chain of ownership.

  3. Step three

    Problems are cleared

    Old mortgages are released, payoffs are ordered, and anything found in the search is resolved before closing day rather than on it.

  4. Step four

    Documents and figures

    The deed is prepared, the lender's package arrives, and the settlement figures are checked against the contract.

  5. Step five

    Closing day

    Everyone signs. Each document is explained. Funds are collected and paid out, and the keys change hands.

  6. Step six

    Recording

    The deed and mortgage are recorded with the county probate office and the title policies are issued.

Wire fraud is aimed at closings. Call before you send money.

Criminals send emails that look like they come from your agent, lender or attorney, with new wiring instructions. Treat any emailed change as fraud until you have confirmed it by voice, using a number you already know.
