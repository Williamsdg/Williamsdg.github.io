Opening a file

What the office needs from you.

The more of this that arrives with the contract, the sooner title work starts.

  • The fully signed contract and every addendum.
  • Names, phone numbers and email addresses for buyers and sellers.
  • The lender, loan officer and processor, or a note that it is a cash sale.
  • Who is holding the earnest money.
  • The seller's current mortgage lender, for the payoff.
  • HOA name and management contact, if there is one.
  • Any survey, termite letter or prior title policy the parties already have.
  • The closing date in the contract, and any dates that cannot move.

Things that hold up a closing

Flag these early and they stop being problems.

A seller who has died or divorced

Title may need an estate opened or a deed from a former spouse. Both take time and neither can be fixed at the table.

Someone signing by power of attorney

The lender has to approve the document before closing, and the original has to be recorded.

A property held by an LLC or trust

The office will need the formation documents showing who has authority to sign.

Out-of-town buyers or sellers

Documents can be signed ahead of time, but originals have to travel. Say so when the file is opened.

Wire fraud is aimed at closings. Call before you send money.

Criminals send emails that look like they come from your agent, lender or attorney, with new wiring instructions. Treat any emailed change as fraud until you have confirmed it by voice, using a number you already know.
