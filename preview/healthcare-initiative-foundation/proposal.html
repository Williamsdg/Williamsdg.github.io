Application

Williams Digital is a small web design and development studio led by Dylan Williams. We would build HIF a WordPress site that staff can update on their own, that tells grant partners' stories with photographs taken in Montgomery County, and that makes the grant calendar the easiest thing to find.

What we noticed on the current site

Before writing this, we read hifmc.org, the FY26 awards announcement, and the FY27 grant cycle newsletter. Three things stood out, and each one shaped the concept we built.

  • Grant dates are inside paragraphs. A partner has to read closely to find when the portal opens and closes. The concept puts a calendar and a live "open or closed" status at the top of the site.
  • Each year's grant recipients are published as images of text. Screen readers and search engines cannot read them. The concept turns them into a searchable directory that staff update in one place.
  • HIF's best stories are hard to find. The three collective impact stories from the 50th anniversary and the partner quotes in press releases sit in older posts. The concept gives them a permanent home.

Experience in website development and design

Dylan designs and builds every site himself and stays on as the support contact after launch. Recent work most relevant to HIF:

  • Vestlake Communities at Liberty Park (vestlakelibertypark.com). A public website for a residential community association: events, newsletters, documents, and resources kept current for residents. It is close in shape to HIF's site, where newsletters, guidelines, and dated announcements all need a clear home.
  • Legacy Sports Media (legacysportshq.com). A public media platform covering Alabama high school athletics, with a custom content system that nontechnical staff use to publish day to day.
  • Vardar Transportation (vardartransportation.com). A company website delivered end to end, from design through domain setup, with ongoing hosting and support.
  • Senn Medicine (under contract, in progress). A new website for Dr. Eric Senn's direct primary care practice in Auburn, Alabama, explaining in plain language how membership care works, what is included, and who is eligible.
  • Buy Auburn (buyauburn.com). A community commerce platform serving Auburn, Alabama, live and supported.

Our sites are hand-coded, mobile-first, and light, with no page-builder layer between staff and their content. For HIF we would stay on WordPress, which your team already knows, and replace the current page builder with a small custom theme. Staff would edit labeled fields ("Grant cycle opens," "Partner name," "Story photo") and the layout would hold.

Experience in communication development

On most projects we write or rewrite the site's words along with the design, because the two cannot be separated. Our method is simple: interview the people closest to the work, find the plain sentence underneath the formal one, and test it on someone outside the organization. For HIF that means a short voice guide (how HIF sounds, words to use and avoid, how to describe a grant in two sentences) and page copy for the mission, vision, guidelines, strategic priorities, and grantmaking calendar, drafted with staff and approved by the board. The concept's "How to apply" section is an example: HIF's own guidelines, rewritten as four steps.

Knowledge of Montgomery County, Maryland

We are based in Alabama, so we will not claim a neighbor's knowledge. Here is what we have learned, and how we would close the gap.

  • The county is large and diverse. More than a million people live in Montgomery County. About a third were born outside the United States, and more than four in ten speak a language other than English at home (U.S. Census Bureau). A site for this community needs plain language, strong mobile performance, and a structure that is ready for translation.
  • Need is not evenly spread. HIF's guidelines weigh the geographic area served, including historically under-invested communities, and recent grants reach from Germantown to Poolesville. Partner stories and photographs should show the whole county, not only its best-known places.
  • HIF convenes as well as funds. Its coalition work with groups such as Leadership Montgomery and the Montgomery Cares Advisory Board is part of the story.
  • How we close the gap. The photographer will be local to Montgomery County. Interviews with board members, staff, and six to eight grant partners come first, before any design. Dylan would also travel to the county for the photography days if HIF would like that (priced as an option in the budget).

Accessibility and security

We build to WCAG 2.2 Level AA: color contrast, keyboard access, visible focus, text alternatives, readable type, and reduced motion. We test with automated tools and by hand with a screen reader, and give HIF a written report. We would meet HIF's IT security team at kickoff, agree on hosting, accounts, backups, and updates in writing, and have them review the site before launch.

HIF owns everything: the theme, source files, copy, photographs, templates, and all accounts.

Communication, design, and questions · Williams DigitalRFP template responses

Communication style

  • You work directly with Dylan from the first call to launch day. There are no account managers and no handoffs.
  • We hold a short check-in every two weeks and send a written recap the same day: what was done, what is next, and what we need from you.
  • We use plain language, and explain choices and trade-offs without jargon.
  • We show working pages early, on a private link, so feedback is about the real thing and not a description of it.
  • Emails are answered within one business day.

Design style

  • People first. Real photographs of real partners carry the site, and stock imagery stays out.
  • The design grows from your logo. The tree and its colors become the system for the site and the social templates.
  • Short pages, plain words, and one clear next step on each.
  • Accessible from the first sketch: contrast, keyboard use, screen readers, and readable type are design decisions, not a final check.
  • Calm and fast. Motion is used only where it helps someone understand something.

Questions for us

  1. Who approves design and copy at each milestone, and how involved would the board like to be between them?
  2. Who is your IT security team, and where is the site hosted today? We would like to meet them at kickoff.
  3. For the Foundant portal, is a clear link enough, or would you like open applications and deadlines shown on the website itself?
  4. Do you already work with a photographer you trust? Which grant partners would be most glad to be photographed?
  5. Would the board consider Spanish or other languages for key grantseeker pages, now or in a later phase?
  6. Which tools do staff use for social graphics today, so the templates arrive where the team already works?
