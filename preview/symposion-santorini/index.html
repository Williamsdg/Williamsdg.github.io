What Symposion is

Nine holes above a winery door. Nine muses.

Symposion is not a museum and not a bar. It is a cultural center inside a turn-of-the-century winery, where ancient Greek music, mythology and Santorinian wine are handed to you in person — by the people who make the instruments.

The premises were built at the turn of the last century by the master artisan and winemaker Zorzis Ioannis Saliveros, in Megalochori — a settlement that dates back to the 17th century. When the founders first saw the nine holes suspended above the main entrance, they read them as the nine muses of the arts, and built the programme around them.

Both music and mythology exist on two plains. The first in the present moment — ephemeral. The second in stasis — immortal.

The Mythical Music Journey

You don’t watch it. You’re in it.

It begins with the building — a working winery a century ago — and ends with instruments in your hands, made in the workshop next door.

  • iThe history of the former winery, told in the room where the wine was pressed.
  • iiThe nine muses, and the myths attached to the instruments you’re about to hear.
  • iiiLive playing on the tsabouna, the pan pipe, the side flutes and the Cycladic drum.
  • ivAn interactive close — the part where the audience becomes the instrument.

The Artisan’s Workshop

Goatskin, cane, bone and horn.

Every instrument used in the presentations and the evening performances is made on site. The workshop is open to walk through, and the instruments are for sale.

Yannis Pantazis makes a remarkable array of traditional instruments alongside instruments of his own design, inspired by mythology and the role of music in Greece’s past. He has developed custom instruments for Vangelis Papathanasiou and Dead Can Dance, and composed for Ridley Scott’s Gladiator II.

  • —Tsabouna, the Greek bagpipe — goatskin, cane and bone
  • —Doubaki, the Cycladic drum — goatskin and wood
  • —Monaulos & Diaulos, single and double reed pipes
  • —Syrinx, the pan pipe — and the double syrinx
  • —Plagiaulos, side flutes — pentatonic and diatonic — and the river flute

The vaulted chamber where the wine was pressed, the bench where the instruments are made, the exhibition, the library, the shop, the garden and the café — all inside the same walls.

How booking works

Four steps, one email.

Symposion confirms every booking personally. There is no queue and no third-party platform — you propose, we reply.

  1. 1

    Choose your experience

    Music Journey, Pan Pipe Workshop, Wine Philosophy, an evening performance, or a private booking.

  2. 2

    Suggest a day and time

    Anything Tuesday to Sunday inside the season. The Music Journey runs at 12:00; the others are arranged around you.

  3. 3

    We reply to confirm

    You get an email back from Symposion with the time held, the meeting point and what to bring. Usually the same day.

  4. 4

    Arrive in Megalochori

    Footsteps from the main square, under the bougainvillea. Come a few minutes early — the café is open before and after.

Optional future extension — not built

If it ever earns its place, an app could follow.

Nothing here exists today, and the website does not depend on it. It is sketched only so the decision stays open — the content already sits in a shape an app could read.
