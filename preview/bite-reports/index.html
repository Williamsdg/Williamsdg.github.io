Two reports a week. Two weeks out.

Deciding to take a Friday off is a decision you make more than three days in advance. So every report runs two weeks, and it says plainly how much to trust each week.

Sundaythe new two-week outlook

The engine crunches weather, pressure, water temps and frontal passage, coupled with moon phase and seasonal patterns, for every lake in your state.

Wednesdaythe midweek refresh

The same calls, re-run against the newest forecast, so the weekend windows you are planning around are built on Wednesday's data, not Sunday's.

Week one: full confidence

Inside the reliable forecast window. Specific day and time calls, by region, with the reason for each.

Week two: an honest early read

Weather models get less reliable past about a week. You still get the second week, labelled for confidence, with directional calls rather than a pretend hour. When the data isn't there, the report says the data isn't there.

Moon phase is one of roughly a dozen things.

Most fishing forecasts show you a moon phase and a fish icon. These are the factors that actually move a crappie to bite, weighed against each other, for specific lakes.

Barometric pressure

Direction and rate of change, day by day — not just today’s reading.

Frontal timing

When the front actually crosses your part of the state.

Wind

Speed and direction, and what that does to your banks.

Moon

Phase plus major and minor periods, weighted against everything else.

Water temperature

Where each lake sits, and where it is heading.

Spawn timing

How far ahead or behind normal your lake is running.

Water movement

Generation and flow on current-driven lakes.

The lake itself

Depth, structure, and how that specific water behaves.

Built region by region. The whole state is not equal.

A front reaching north Alabama Tuesday afternoon may not clear the southern end until Wednesday night, which shifts every window with it. Reports are built lake by lake, with that regional lag accounted for.
