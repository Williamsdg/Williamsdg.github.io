1.The short version

Every athlete on the Lattlis roster is at a different point in their career, which means a single athlete template would serve all of them badly. A high school quarterback trying to get recruited and a professional lineman building a sponsorship brand need almost nothing in common on a website.

So this proposal does two things. First, it gives each of the ten athletes its own creative direction — a concept, a brand idea, and the specific sections that athlete's audience actually needs. Second, it recommends building three pilot sites before committing to all ten, chosen so that the three together prove out the entire athlete-development pipeline.

Every site is built on the same technical foundation, carries its own personalized domain, and links back to Lattlis as the central agency hub. Lattlis stays the front door; each athlete gets a destination that belongs to them.

Before anything is built Nothing on this page states a verified fact about any athlete. Schools, jersey numbers, measurements, statistics, offers, eligibility, current team, and playing status are all marked To confirm and must be confirmed by Lattlis or the athlete before development begins. The nicknames and taglines below are proposals, not decisions — each one needs the athlete's approval before it goes on a site.

Personalized domain per athlete
Each athlete gets their own address — not a subpage of the agency site. It survives a transfer, a signing, or a change in representation structure.
Lattlis stays the hub
Every athlete site carries Lattlis branding, a link back to the agency, and routes serious inquiries through Lattlis contact channels.
One shared codebase
Film embeds, stat tables, timelines, media kits, and contact forms are built once and reused. Adding athlete eleven costs a fraction of athlete one.
Ten distinct identities
Shared plumbing, separate design. No two athletes get the same layout, palette, typography, or section order.
Built to be updated
Offers, stats, film, schedules, and status change constantly. Updating them is a content change, not a rebuild.
Phone first
Coaches, scouts, and sponsors open these on a phone between meetings. Mobile is the primary design target, not an afterthought.

5.Recommended rollout

Three pilots before ten builds

We would not build all ten previews before Lattlis commits. Three initial sites prove the system, establish the shared foundation, and give Lattlis something real to show the rest of the roster — at a fraction of the cost and calendar time of a full rollout.

Pilot 1 — Jeremiah Lattier · high school recruiting

Pilot 2 — Davion Grant · college recruiting, transfer, and NIL

Pilot 3 — Ladarian Paulk or Davone Walden Jr. · professional transition

Those three cover the entire athlete-development pipeline, which is the real argument to the rest of the roster: whatever stage an athlete is at, Lattlis has already built the site for it.

Stage 1
High school recruiting
Offers, film, camps, academics — built for college coaches
Stage 2
College & NIL
Production, awards, media kit — built for programs and brands
Stage 3
Transfer opportunities
Eligibility, availability, film — built for the next program
Stage 4
Professional portfolio
Pro Day, résumé, scouting profile — built for teams and agents

Highlighted stages are covered by the three recommended pilots. Stage 3 is covered by Davion's transfer module and is fully built out when Alvaro Ortega-Morales or Henry Tolliver comes online.

Once the system is established, the remaining seven athletes reuse the same technical foundation while receiving completely different branding, content, and features. Lattlis remains the central agency hub, and each athlete receives a personalized domain connected back to it.

6.What we need confirmed before development

This is the one thing that will slow the project down if it is not handled first. An athlete site loses its credibility the moment a coach or scout spots a wrong number. We will not publish a statistic, measurement, offer, or status that Lattlis or the athlete has not confirmed.

School, team, and current playing status for each athlete — including anyone currently between programs
Remaining eligibility where it is relevant to the athlete's objective
Verified measurements — height, weight, and any combine or Pro Day testing numbers, with the source of each
Career and season statistics with the source we should cite
Offers, honors, and awards — only those that can be documented
Jersey numbers for any monogram or numeral used in branding
Film links — Hudl, YouTube, or raw files, and who owns them
Photography — existing usable images, or a plan for a shoot
Nickname and tagline approval from each athlete, in their own words
Contact routing — which inquiries go to Lattlis, which go to a parent, which go to the athlete
Minors: written parent or guardian approval for every element of Justin Lattier's site, and for any other athlete under 18
Domain preferences for each athlete, with two alternates in case the first is taken
On school marks and trademarks No site will use a school's logo, wordmark, or protected color-and-mark combination. Where a direction references school colors, we build a palette inspired by them that belongs to the athlete. This keeps every site usable after a transfer and avoids licensing problems.

7.What every build includes

Personalized visual identity
Palette, typography, monogram or wordmark, and layout designed for that athlete alone.
Film room
Hudl and video embeds organized by season, opponent, or skill — not a wall of unlabeled links.
Statistics and measurables
Clean, sourced tables that read correctly on a phone.
Career timeline
Where they have been, what they did there, and what comes next.
Contact routing
Inquiry forms and direct contact that land where Lattlis wants them, with spam protection.
Downloadable one-pager
A print-ready athlete résumé or media kit generated from the same information as the site.
Domain and hosting
Personalized domain, SSL, and hosting configured and connected back to the Lattlis hub.
Update path
A defined process for changing offers, stats, film, schedule, and status during the season.

8.How it would run

Phase 1
Confirmation
Lattlis returns the verification checklist for the three pilot athletes. Nothing is designed until the facts are settled. Nicknames and taglines go to the athletes for approval.
Phase 2
Direction & design
One design direction per pilot athlete, presented for review. Each athlete sees their own concept before it is built.
Phase 3
Build the foundation
The shared component library — film room, stat tables, timeline, media kit, contact routing — built once against the first pilot, then reused.
Phase 4
Three pilots live
All three sites launched on their own domains, connected back to Lattlis, tested on phones first. Lattlis has something real to show the rest of the roster.
Phase 5
Roster rollout
The remaining seven athletes come online in whatever order serves their careers — signing windows, transfer portals, and Pro Days set the priority, not the list order.
