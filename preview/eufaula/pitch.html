The lake is the brand. The website should act like it.

Lake Eufaula draws anglers, boaters, and lake-home buyers from three states — and Alabama's biggest historic district seals the trip. None of it shows at the city's digital front door. Here's what a lake-first municipal site looks like, and why it pays for itself.

The Opportunity

Four things holding the current site back

01

The lake brand is invisible

The Big Bass Capital of the World with a website that could belong to any inland town. The single most marketable asset in Barbour County goes unused online.

02

Visitor info is scattered

Fishing reports on forums, ramp info on state pages, lodging on booking apps, Pilgrimage on a separate site. Nobody assembles the lake weekend — which is exactly what the city's site should do.

03

Economic development is buried

Waterfront businesses, downtown storefronts, and lake-economy investors should see opportunity in seconds. Today that case is scattered or missing — and it's exactly where cities win grants.

04

The visuals feel dated

A city selling water, mansions, and sunsets with small static pages. Perception matters — to visiting anglers, to relocating retirees, and to businesses comparing towns.

More Than a Facelift

What a modern municipal site actually delivers

The visible redesign is the easy part. These are the points that matter to stakeholders, auditors, and grant applications:

ADA Accessible

Built to WCAG 2.1 AA — screen-reader friendly, keyboard navigable, proper contrast. Government sites are held to this standard, and non-compliance is a legal risk.

Mobile First

Anglers check conditions from the truck and residents pay bills from a phone. Every page is designed for a phone screen first, desktop second.

Fast Loading

Lightweight pages that load in under two seconds — including on the water and in rural Barbour County.

Staff-Editable

City staff post news, alerts, and lake conditions themselves — no developer, no waiting. A ramp closure goes up in minutes.

The Payoff

Benefits for the City

Beyond the new look, the redesign changes how the city operates day to day:

  • Mobile-friendly for residents — services work from any phone
  • Faster access to city services — pay, report, and apply in fewer clicks
  • Easier navigation for permits and forms — less counter traffic, fewer calls to City Hall
  • ADA-conscious design — accessible to every resident, and defensible
  • Tournament and visitor traffic captured — more meals, more nights, more lodging tax
  • The lake economy presented to investors and site selectors
  • Improved community engagement — events, alerts, and council business residents actually see
Helping Eufaula Grow

An investment that works for the city

A municipal website isn't an expense line — it's infrastructure for the four things every lake town is trying to do:

Tourism Promotion

Anglers and boaters already come. A site that assembles the weekend keeps them downtown for dinner and a night in the historic district.

Business Attraction

The lake economy attracts marinas, outfitters, restaurants, and lodging. Show them the traffic, the property, and a named contact.

Community Engagement

Events, meetings, and city news residents actually find — participation goes up when information is easy.

Resident Services

Bills paid, issues reported, permits started online — around the clock, without a trip to City Hall.
