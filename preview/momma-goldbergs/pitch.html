The Gap

Where the current site leaves money on the table

1 · The homepage doesn't sell

Today

Logo, basic navigation, generic photos, and no strong call to action. Nothing steams, nothing moves, nothing says "order now."

The Concept

A hero built around the Momma's Love and the 50th anniversary, with three actions above the fold: Order Online, Find a Location, Join Rewards.

2 · The menu is an image

Today

The menu page is literally a JPG — invisible to search engines, unreadable on phones, impossible to update per store.

The Concept

A searchable, categorized menu with "Most Ordered" and "Auburn Favorite" badges — the experience Jersey Mike's and Chick-fil-A customers expect.

3 · Ten locations, one flat list

Today

Location discovery is buried. Each store's hours, ordering, and specials are hard to find or missing.

The Concept

"Find Your Momma G's" — filterable AL/GA finder where every store has hours, ordering links, catering contact, and its own specials, managed from one dashboard.

4 · Catering is treated as a footnote

Today

A single page pointing at EZCater. No packages, no pricing guidance, no reason to commit.

The Concept

A Catering Hub: tailgates, corporate lunches, church events, graduations — with a headcount calculator that produces an instant estimate and a quote request.

5 · The 1976 story is untold

Today

Fifty years of Auburn history reduced to one paragraph. This is the single biggest missed brand asset.

The Concept

"Auburn Through the Years" — a heritage timeline of photos, former Auburn athletes, and fan memories that no competitor can copy.

6 · The app is a side note, and game day doesn't exist

Today

Two small app badges. No loyalty story, no push strategy, no acknowledgment that Auburn football happens.

The Concept

Rewards featured as the front door — points, birthday rewards, game-day offers — plus a seasonal Game Day Mode with tailgate packages, order cutoffs, and pickup windows.
