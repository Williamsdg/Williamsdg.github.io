Huddle With Coach Pink

A film studio for Auburn football. Mark every play, draw on the frame, log what happened, and loop the play back. This guide walks through the whole workflow — built for the person breaking film down for the first time.

01 The big picture

What the tool is, who it's for, and the three ways to load a game.

Huddle With Coach Pink turns any Auburn game video into a structured film breakdown. You watch the game once, mark each play as you go, and end up with a sortable log of every snap — with notes, drawings, stats, and tags — that you (or anyone you send the file to) can replay later.

It runs entirely in your browser. No login. No account. Everything you build is saved to your browser automatically, and you can export the breakdown as a file to send to someone else.

Three video sources

SourceWhat it's good forLimitations
YouTube Highlight reels, full-game uploads — the fastest way to start No frame-step. WebM clip export not available.
MP4 upload Local game film. Full control: frame-step, WebM clip export. Video file isn't saved between sessions — you re-attach it on reload.
Embed URL Hudl, Vimeo, or any iframe-able player No programmatic control — timestamps come from a stopwatch you start manually.
💡
Which one to use?If you have access to coaches' film as an MP4 file, that's the best experience. YouTube works great for highlight tape and broadcast cuts. Hudl/Vimeo embeds are the fallback when neither of the others is an option.

03 Starting a new game

Get a video loaded in under 30 seconds.

  1. Click + New Game in the top bar.

    A modal opens. Type the opponent name, set the date, season, and home/away.

  2. Pick a video source.

    Three tabs: YouTube, Upload MP4, or Embed URL. Pick the one you have, paste/upload, and the modal validates it on submit.

  3. Hit Create Game.

    The video loads and the empty-game state disappears. You're ready to mark plays.

📌
You can have many games saved.The dropdown at the top of the screen lets you switch between them. Every game keeps its own plays, drawings, and stats.

04 Marking a play

The core loop. Two ways to do it — pick whichever fits the moment.

Method A: Mark a play as a range (recommended)

This is what you want for film breakdown — the play has a start (the snap) and an end (the whistle), so it can be looped and re-watched cleanly later.

  1. Find the snap.

    Use the scrubber or arrow keys. Pause exactly when the ball is snapped.

  2. Press I (or click [ IN).

    Locks the in-point. You'll see "IN: 2:08" in the readout under the controls.

  3. Play forward to the whistle.

    Press Space or L until the play ends, then pause.

  4. Press O (or click OUT ]).

    Locks the out-point. Readout now shows IN and OUT.

  5. Press M (or click ⦿ Mark Play).

    The play is captured as a range. IN/OUT clear automatically so the next play starts fresh.

What gets captured
I (2:08) snap
O (2:14) whistle
The orange band is the play. Clicking this play later will seek to 2:08 (paused), and pressing play loops 2:08 → 2:14 forever until you navigate away.

Method B: Mark a single timestamp (faster, no range)

If you just want a bookmark at a specific moment — pre-snap motion, a sideline reaction, a halftime adjustment note — skip the IN/OUT and just press M. You'll get a single-point marker with no loop range. You can always add a range later from the Play Details pane using Set OUT here.

⚡
You can also use the buttons.Everything has a clickable equivalent — IN, OUT, Mark Play, prev/next — but once you get a rhythm going, the keyboard is dramatically faster. The I → play → O → M combo becomes muscle memory in about 5 plays.

05 Where plays go

The left sidebar is your play log.

Every play you mark appears in the Plays list on the left, sorted by time. Each item shows three things:

  • Timestamp chip — orange and showing a range like 2:08–2:14 if you marked it as a range. Navy and showing a single time like 1:15 if it's a point.
  • Play-type chip — OFF (offense), DEF (defense), or ST (special teams).
  • Title — auto-generated based on the timestamp; you can edit it in Play Details.

Click any play to load it. The video seeks to the start, the right panel fills in with that play's details, and any drawings you made on that play come back.

Navigating between plays

  • [ — previous play
  • ] — next play
  • Or click the ⟨⟨ / ⟩⟩ buttons next to the play button

Search

The search box at the top of the Plays panel filters the list by title or notes. Useful when you've got 80 plays and want to find "3rd down" or "RPO".

06 Logging what happened

Per-play details, schemas, tags — the structured side.

When a play is active, the Play Details tab on the right fills in. The most important part: play type. The schema changes based on what you pick.

TypeHotkeyWhat you fill in
Offense 1 Down, distance, yard line, hash, personnel, formation, play type (run/pass/RPO/etc.), concept, result yards, motion. Success and explosive flags auto-calculate.
Defense 2 Down, distance, front, coverage, blitz, blitzer, result yards allowed, plus TFL / sack / PBU / INT / missed-tackle flags.
Special teams 3 Phase (kickoff/punt/FG/PAT/onside), result, return yards.

Success and explosive — what the auto-calc means

When you enter result yards on an offensive play, the app calculates two flags automatically:

  • Success — 50% of the line to gain on 1st down, 70% on 2nd, 100% on 3rd or 4th. The Auburn analytics standard.
  • Explosive — 12+ yards on a run, 16+ yards on a pass. Same standard.

Tags

Below the schema you'll see a Tags row. Type a tag like trips-right or play-action and hit Enter. Tags show up as chip × pills you can click to remove. Tags also build an autocomplete list — once you've used play-action on three plays, the suggestion appears as you type on the fourth. The Stats tab can filter by tag, so you can answer questions like "what's our success rate on play-action?"

Concept autocomplete

The offensive Concept field has the same memory. Once you type Mesh for the first time, it'll suggest itself the next time you tab into Concept on a different play.

✏️
Notes are free-form.The textarea at the bottom of Play Details is for anything that doesn't fit a field — read keys, coaching points, broadcast quotes, whatever. They're searchable from the Plays sidebar.

07 Drawing on the frame

Telestrate routes, coverage shells, gap fits — directly on the video.

The dark strip below the transport controls is the telestrator. Pause the video, pick a tool, and drag on the frame.

Tools

  • Pen ✎ — freehand line. Good for routes and motion paths.
  • Arrow ↗ — directional pointer. Click-drag, arrowhead lands at the release.
  • Circle ○ — ellipse. Drag the bounding box. Good for marking a player.
  • Rectangle ▭ — boxes. Useful for highlighting a zone or coverage area.
  • Text A — prompts you for the string, drops it where you clicked.

Color & width

Five preset swatches (Auburn orange, white, yellow, red, cyan) plus a custom color picker. The thickness slider next to them controls stroke width. Picking a swatch always shows the active one with a white outline.

Undo, redo, clear

Standard. Drawings auto-save to the active play, so undo and redo persist across marker switches — click a different play and come back, and your undo stack is right where you left it. Note: drawings on legacy plays (saved before the editable-strokes upgrade) appear as a flat backdrop and aren't undoable — clear and redraw to make them editable.

Auto-clear on play / Pin to clip

Two checkboxes on the right side of the telestrator strip:

  • Auto-clear on play — when playback resumes, drawings hide. When you pause again, they come back. Mimics how broadcast telestrators work.
  • Pin to clip — overrides auto-clear when you're inside a clip range. Drawings stay visible while you loop a clip, so you can show the same diagram on every replay.

Snapshot

The 📷 button exports the current frame + drawings as a PNG. The filename includes the opponent, quarter, and play title, so files come out like huddle-snap-alabama-q3-3rd-and-7-mesh-2m15.png. YouTube and Hudl/Vimeo limitation: only the drawing layer exports (cross-origin blocks pulling pixels from those players). MP4 sources get the full frame.

08 Reviewing plays later

The auto-loop is the magic of the tool.

Once a play is marked as a range (with IN and OUT), clicking it works like this:

  1. Seek to IN, stay paused.

    The video jumps to the snap and stops there. You can frame-step around the pre-snap look or just sit on the formation.

  2. Press Space to start.

    Playback runs forward.

  3. The play loops automatically.

    When it hits OUT, the video jumps back to IN and replays. Forever, until you click a different play or scrub away.

Slow it down

The speed dropdown in the transport strip goes from 0.25× to 2×. 0.5× is the sweet spot for watching footwork and reads. Works on MP4 and YouTube.

Export a clip

On MP4 sources, the ⤓ WebM button records the current IN→OUT range to a downloadable WebM file. You'll be asked if you want to burn the drawings into the export — useful for sending a coach-edited clip to staff. YouTube and iframe sources can't be re-encoded, so the button is grayed out there; for those, just save the IN/OUT timestamps as a named clip in the Clips list and the viewer can replay it natively.

Adjusting a play's range

Sometimes the snap or whistle was off. With the play active, look at the Play Details pane:

  • The −½  −⅒  +⅒  +½ buttons next to the Start (IN) time nudge the whole range by ±0.1s or ±0.5s. The play duration stays the same — both ends shift together.
  • Set OUT here below the time row replaces the end of the play with the current playback position. Useful for trimming the whistle in.
  • Clear removes the range and turns the play back into a single-timestamp marker.

09 Saving your work

Auto-save, export, import, and the MP4 re-attach banner.

Auto-save

Everything saves to your browser's localStorage automatically — every play, every drawing, every note. There's no save button. The status is invisible unless something goes wrong.

⚠
localStorage is per-browser and per-device.If you build a breakdown on your laptop, you won't see it on your iPad unless you export the file and import it on the other device. Same if you switch from Chrome to Safari, or if you clear your browser data.

Export

Top bar → Export. Downloads a JSON file like huddle-Alabama-2025-11-29.json with every play, every drawing, every stat, every tag. Email it, drop it in a shared folder, AirDrop it, whatever.

Import

Top bar → Import → pick a JSON file. It loads as a new game (gets a fresh internal ID, so importing an existing breakdown won't overwrite anything). Switch to it from the game dropdown.

MP4 re-attach

If your game uses an MP4 source, the breakdown persists across sessions but the video file itself doesn't (it'd be too big for localStorage). On reload, you'll see a gold banner: "Re-attach video for filename.mp4" with a file picker. Pick the same file and your breakdown reattaches to it.

YouTube and iframe games don't need this — they reload from the URL.

11 Troubleshooting

What the common error states mean and how to fix them.

"This YouTube video disallows embedding"

Some YouTube uploaders disable playback on other websites. The toast appears with error code 101 or 150. There's nothing you can do from this side — try a different upload of the same game (Auburn highlight reels usually have a few mirrors), or download the MP4 and use the upload path.

The MP4 re-attach banner won't go away

This is normal until you re-pick the file. Browsers don't let websites hold onto file references across reloads for security reasons. Click Choose file in the banner and pick the same MP4.

⚠
File name doesn't have to match perfectlybut if the duration is wildly different from the original, your play timestamps will be off. The app loosely validates the filename and warns you on mismatch but doesn't block — your call.

"This game is over 4MB"

You've made a lot of telestrator drawings, and they're starting to bump against browser storage limits. Export the game to a JSON file, then delete it from the app and re-import from the file if you need to keep editing. Or just keep exporting periodically as a backup.

Drawings disappeared after switching plays

Shouldn't happen anymore — drawings persist as editable strokes per play. However: plays created before the May 2026 upgrade were saved as flattened images, not editable strokes. They show up as a static backdrop you can't undo. To make a legacy play editable: hit Clear and redraw, or just leave it as a backdrop.

Drawings vanish during playback and come back when I pause

That's the "Auto-clear on play" toggle (telestrator strip, right side). Uncheck it to keep drawings visible during playback. Or check "Pin to clip" if you want them visible only while looping a clip.

Stats numbers look wrong

Check the filters at the top of the Stats tab — Quarter, Down, and Tag. If any of them is set, you're seeing the filtered slice, not the totals. Set them all to "All" to see overall numbers.

iframe (Hudl/Vimeo) timestamps are weird

Iframe sources can't be controlled programmatically, so the app uses a wall-clock stopwatch (bottom right of the screen) instead. You have to start it manually when the video begins, and timestamps come from that stopwatch — not from the embedded player's playhead. If you scrub around in the embed, the stopwatch won't follow. For serious film breakdown, prefer the YouTube or MP4 path.
