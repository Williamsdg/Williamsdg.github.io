What matters most

Four things a city website has to get right

These matter more than how a site looks.

Accessibility

Federal rules now require city websites to meet WCAG 2.1 AA. The City's own agenda puts the deadline at April 2028. Meeting the standard is a matter of how the site is built, not an upgrade bought later.

Phones first

Most residents open the site on a phone, often in a parking lot or a checkout line. Every screen of the concept was designed at phone width first and then widened.

Speed

A light page loads on a weak signal and on an older phone. The concept is a small fraction of the current home page's size.

Staff can update it

Notices, meeting dates, schedules and documents are changed by City Hall staff in a simple editor. No vendor ticket for day-to-day updates, and notices expire on their own.

Event websites

A page of its own for each event that draws a crowd

Montevallo runs more events than most cities its size. Today each one lives in a Facebook post and a line on the annual events page. A dedicated page gives visitors the schedule and map, gives vendors the rules and the form, and gives sponsors a place to be seen. Built in days, reused every year.

Benefits for the City

Less time on the phone, fewer trips to the counter

  • Fewer "which day is garbage?" and "how do I pay this?" calls. The answers are the first thing on the page and the search finds them by plain words.
  • Nothing stale on the home page. Notices and events carry dates and drop off on their own, so no one has to remember to take down last year's election page.
  • One entry, three places. A calendar item shows on the home page, the events page and in the text alerts.
  • Event questions go to the event page, not to the Clerk's line. Vendor rules, maps and sponsor details are written once.
  • Accessibility handled in the build, not as a paid upgrade on a renewal.
  • Room to grow. Agendas with searchable text, online forms that submit instead of print, and a vendor and sponsor portal for Main Street can be added when the City is ready.

Helping Montevallo grow

The first thing a business owner sees

A family deciding whether to move to Montevallo, a UM graduate thinking about staying, or someone looking at a storefront on Main Street will open the City's website before they call anyone.

Business attraction

Start-a-business steps, the license application, bid opportunities and the Industrial Development Board, with I-65 and rail access, on one page.

Visitors and events

The American Village and the Alabama National Cemetery bring more than 100,000 visitors a year. ArtWalk, Tinglewood, Monte Gras and the Cove give them a reason to stay for dinner.

A college town

About 2,400 students and 700 employees at the University of Montevallo. The site shows a city that is easy to live in, not only to study in.

Resident services

The everyday tasks stay first, so the site works for the people who already live here.
