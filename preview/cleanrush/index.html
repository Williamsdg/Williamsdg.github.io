8 conversion opportunities on cleanrush.app today.

What the current site does well and the specific gaps this redesign addresses. Findings pulled directly from the live site's HTML, structured data, and visible UX.

1

No marketing layer above the booking flow.

The site lands users directly into the app. A homepage that sells trust before the form would meaningfully reduce bounce on cold paid traffic.

2

Google Ads conversion ID is still a placeholder.

The gtag snippet in your index.html reads AW-XXXXXXXXXX. Any paid traffic right now is uncounted — campaigns can't optimize blind.

3

No public pricing or instant-quote preview.

Visitors have to commit to the booking flow to see a number. An instant quote widget (like the one in the hero above) qualifies traffic in seconds.

4

No social proof on the homepage.

Your FAQ schema is strong, but cleaning is a trust purchase. Pull your top Google reviews above the fold — auto-refreshed monthly.

5

13 cities in schema, zero location pages.

You declared every city in your service area in structured data, but there's no /houston-cleaning, /katy-cleaning, etc. for local SEO to rank on.

6

No lead capture for visitors who don't book.

The vast majority of cold traffic isn't ready to book on visit one. A "$25 off → text me my code" capture turns those visitors into a remarketing list.

7

No before/after or photo gallery.

Cleaning sells on visuals. A small before/after grid (10–15 jobs) is one of the highest-converting sections we can add — and ranks in Google Images for "house cleaning Houston."

8

No retention engine beyond the booking app.

The biggest revenue lift for a cleaning brand isn't acquisition — it's repeat. We mocked an ops dashboard with SMS reminders, review automation, repeat-booking campaigns, and a referral loop. See the demo →
