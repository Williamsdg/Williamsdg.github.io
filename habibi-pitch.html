The Rhythm
Weekly Schedule
Three streams, two uploads, two to three Shorts. Designed to be sustainable for a first-time streamer while maintaining consistent output.
DayTypeDurationDetails
Sunday Upload — Flagship YouTube video (10-18 min). Best content from the week, fully edited with narrative, overlays, and music. Peak audience day — algorithm pushes into Mon/Tue.
Monday Prep — Review last week's streams. Flag best moments. Plan Thursday's content stream — theme, guest list, structure.
Tuesday Stream 2-3 hrs Chill Night. Survival games or sports games. Low-pressure, chat-focused. The "hang out" stream.
Wednesday Upload — Clip compilation or short-form video (5-8 min). Midweek content boost — fills the gap between streams.
Thursday Stream 2-3 hrs Content Stream. Big Brother sessions, Minecraft events, themed challenges. The most important stream of the week — high-energy, clippable, structured.
Friday Edit — Edit Sunday's flagship video from Thursday footage. Post 1 Short.
Saturday Stream 3-4 hrs Variety / Collab. Sports games, Minecraft builds, survival games, or collabs with other streamers. Longest regular stream — weekend audience. Last Saturday of the month becomes the Charity Stream (6-8 hrs).

Signature Series
Big Brother Minecraft
Structured like a TV show. Every season has an arc, every episode ends with a reason to come back. 4-6 weeks per season, played out on Thursday content streams.

Season Arc

Week 1 — Premiere

Meet the Houseguests

14 players enter the house. First HOH comp, first nominations, first eviction vote. End with: "Next week — the first blindside."

Week 2 — Early Game

Alliances Form

First betrayal. Veto comp introduced. Back-door attempt. End with a cliffhanger vote or secret twist reveal.

Week 3 — Mid Game

The House Splits

Lines are drawn. Double eviction — two go home in one night. End with: "The alliance is broken."

Week 4 — Late Game

Paranoia Peaks

Final 5-6. Diary room confessionals get personal. End with the biggest betrayal of the season.

Week 5 — Finale

Winner Crowned

Final HOH. Final 2 plead their case. Jury votes. Post-game reactions and reunion.

Episode Format

0-2 min

Cold Open

Recap of last episode. "Previously on Big Brother..." with text overlays.

2-22 min

House Life

Players interact, alliances scheme, diary room confessionals. Let the drama breathe.

22-42 min

Competition

HOH, Veto, or special challenge. Obstacle courses, trivia, PvP arenas, build battles.

42-52 min

Nominations

HOH nominates 2 players. Speeches. Reactions. The tension builds.

52-62 min

The Vote

Live eviction vote. Evicted player's exit interview.

62-64 min

Cliffhanger

Tease next week: a twist, a secret alliance reveal, a new comp preview. Always end with a hook.

Twists & Gimmicks

Secret Pairs — two players secretly allied
The Mole — one player sabotages from within
Pandora's Box — power or punishment
Battle Back — evicted player can re-enter
Audience Vote — Twitch chat decides a twist
Hidden Immunity — hidden item saves you
Team Swap — midseason alliance breaker

Players wake up stranded in an unknown world. A mysterious signal is broadcasting from coordinates nobody can reach yet. To unlock the path, the group must complete community milestones. But resources are scarce and not everyone can survive.

Why People Come Back

The Mystery

Every season has a central question that doesn't get answered until the finale. Tease it every episode.

Personal Quests

Each player gets a secret objective — "build a hidden vault," "betray someone by episode 3." Viewers follow their favorites.

Cliffhangers

End every stream mid-moment. Never resolve the tension on the same episode. "We'll find out next Tuesday."

Scarcity Rules

Limited resources, limited lives (3 per season), limited inventory. Scarcity equals tension equals content.

Community Milestones

The group must hit goals together to unlock the next phase. Fail and consequences happen — mob raids, lost items.

Viewer Influence

Twitch chat votes on in-game events. "Supply drop or creeper wave?" Keeps live viewers engaged.

Playbook
Growth Tactics
Borrowed from Ludwig, Karl, and Squeeze. Adapted for a first-time creator starting from zero.
💬

Discord First

Create the Discord before you start streaming. Announce streams, post clips, let people talk. The community is the product — not the game.

🤝

Collab Sideways

Reach out to streamers your size or slightly bigger. Big Brother Minecraft is a natural collab magnet — it needs 6-14 players. Don't chase huge creators. Build together.

🎨

Thumbnails > Everything

Titles and thumbnails matter more than the video itself. Every video should be watchable by someone who's never seen the stream. Hook in 15 seconds.

⚡

Shorts = Growth Engine

Clip the reaction, not the gameplay. Faces over kills. Text overlays on every Short. Post between 11am-1pm or 5pm-7pm for best reach.

🔥

Inside Jokes > Polish

Let bits develop naturally on stream. The community will do the marketing. Chat interaction is the product — read chat constantly, use names, remember regulars.

🌐

YouTube Grows, Twitch Retains

Never skip uploads for streams. YouTube reaches new viewers. Twitch turns them into community members. You need both working together.

Guardrails
What Not to Do
First-timer traps that kill channels before they start.
×

Don't stream 5+ days a week. You'll burn out in 2 months. 3 days is sustainable and leaves time for editing.

×

Don't skip uploads for streams. YouTube grows your audience. Twitch retains it. You need both.

×

Don't over-edit early. A good 12-minute video beats a perfect 20-minute video that took 40 hours.

×

Don't compare your month 1 to their year 5. Ludwig streamed for years before the subathon. Karl was making content long before MrBeast. Consistency is the only cheat code.

×

Don't neglect the Big Brother series. It's your unique angle. Nobody else is doing this the way you are. That's your moat.
