Our Mission

The Birmingham Zoo is Alabama's ONLY institution accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA) — meeting the highest standards in animal care, conservation, education, and guest experience. Operating as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit since 2001, every visit, membership, and donation directly supports our work.

Under the leadership of President & CEO Chris Pfefferkorn — who brings more than 25 years of zoological experience and has guided the zoo since 2018 — our mission is simple and unwavering: to inspire passion to conserve the natural world. We pursue this through world-class animal care, hands-on education, scientific research, and direct participation in global conservation programs that protect wildlife and wild places for future generations.

Home to more than 900 animals representing 195+ species across 122 acres, the Birmingham Zoo has been a cornerstone of conservation and education in the Southeast since 1955. Each year we welcome 644,667 visitors, connecting them to the wonder of the natural world. As a proud member of the American Association of Zoo Keepers, our professional staff upholds the highest standards in zoological care.

Conservation Programs

The Birmingham Zoo actively participates in coordinated conservation efforts that make a tangible difference for endangered and threatened species around the world. Through the AZA's Species Survival Plans (SSPs) and collaborative breeding programs, we help maintain genetically diverse, healthy populations of at-risk animals.

Species Survival Plans

We participate in dozens of AZA Species Survival Plans — scientifically managed breeding programs that maintain genetically healthy, self-sustaining populations of threatened and endangered species across accredited institutions.

Endangered Species Protection

From black rhinos and African elephants to red pandas and sea lions, the Birmingham Zoo cares for numerous species classified as vulnerable, threatened, or endangered. Our expert staff provides individualized care plans and contributes data to global conservation databases.

Elephant Bachelor Herd

The Birmingham Zoo is home to the only all-male African elephant bachelor herd in a U.S. zoo — Bulwagi, Ingadze, and Lutsandvo. This revolutionary program provides a social environment for male African elephants, addressing a critical need in zoo-based elephant management and generating groundbreaking data for elephant conservation research.

Eastern Black Rhino Breeding

Our eastern black rhino breeding program contributes to the survival of one of the most critically endangered large mammals on earth. With fewer than 6,000 black rhinos remaining in the wild, every successful birth is a victory for the species, and the Birmingham Zoo plays a vital role in these efforts.

Species Survival Plan Successes

Red panda cubs born to parents Gizmo and Kodo mark a Species Survival Plan success, while baby giraffe Mopane — born in April 2024 — brings hope for a species whose wild populations are declining. Our cougar rescue program gave Juniper (orphaned in Montana) and Spruce (rescued in California) a permanent home, alongside rescued bobcats Baxter and Boomer — animals that cannot return to the wild.

Rescue & Habitat Conservation

Beyond our gates, we support habitat conservation and field programs that protect wildlife in their native ranges. Here at home, we provide sanctuary for rescued animals like cougars Juniper and Spruce and bobcats Baxter and Boomer, ensuring that species have a future both in the wild and in human care.

Community Science

Conservation starts with curiosity. The Birmingham Zoo empowers visitors of all ages to become active participants in science and wildlife stewardship through community science programs, hands-on events, and citizen-powered data collection.

Community Science Weekend

The Birmingham Zoo's first-ever citizen science event — a two-day celebration of discovery for the whole family. Participate in real data-collection activities, meet researchers, explore nature stations throughout the zoo, and learn how everyday observations contribute to scientific understanding and species protection.

Citizen Science Programs

From bird counts and pollinator surveys to wildlife monitoring, the Birmingham Zoo offers year-round opportunities for guests and volunteers to contribute meaningful data to local and national conservation research efforts.

Outdoor Discovery

Get hands-on with the natural world through guided nature walks, habitat explorations, and seasonal programming that connects families to the ecosystems and wildlife right here in Alabama. Discover how local conservation protects the species in our own backyard.

Interested in getting involved? Whether you want to volunteer with our conservation team, participate in a community science event, or support our mission with a donation, there are many ways to make a difference.
